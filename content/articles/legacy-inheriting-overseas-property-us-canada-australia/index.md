# 海外房产继承：美国/加拿大/澳洲房产如何过户？

父母或配偶在海外留下了一套房产——洛杉矶的公寓、温哥华的联排、悉尼的别墅。人已经去世，国内的继承人却连门都进不去：没有当地法院出具的文件，银行、物业公司、产权登记处都不认你。房子空着，物业费照缴，地税滞纳金越滚越多，个别州甚至可能把欠税房产送入拍卖程序。很多人误以为"人死了房子自然归继承人"，但在普通法国家，没有完成遗嘱认证（Probate），产权在法律上根本不会自动到你名下。拖得越久，成本越高。

## 第1步：确认身份文件与遗嘱（1–4周）

先把"底牌"整理清楚：

- 死亡证明（在当地出具；如需在中国境内使用，通常要办海牙认证 Apostille）；
- 遗嘱原件（如有）——在哪里订立、是否已经过认证程序；
- 继承人与被继承人的亲属关系证明（结婚证、出生证明、户口本等）。

## 第2步：启动遗嘱认证或遗产管理程序（3–12个月）

- 有遗嘱：遗嘱执行人向房产所在地法院申请 Probate，法院确认遗嘱有效后授予执行令；
- 无遗嘱：法院指定遗产管理人（Administrator），按当地无遗嘱继承规则分配；
- 这一步必须在房产所在地法院进行。中国国内的继承权公证书在普通法国家通常不能直接替代当地法院程序。

## 第3步：委托当地律师与税务评估（与第2步并行）

跨境房产继承几乎都需要当地执业律师和会计师：律师负责法院程序与过户，会计师负责遗产税、资本利得税和土地转让税申报。三地差异很大：

- **美国**：联邦遗产税对非居民也有免税额度（2026年约1,300万美元，具体以当年规定为准），部分州还另征州遗产税或继承税；
- **加拿大**：没有遗产税，但去世时视同按市价处置资产（deemed disposition），可能产生资本利得税；
- **澳大利亚**：无遗产税，但继承后出售房产可能涉及资本利得税。

以上金额与规则请以当地当年法律和专业意见为准。

## 第4步：缴纳税费并取得清税证明（2–6个月）

各国要求不一：有的需在过户前缴清遗产税，有的允许分期或延期。美国通常要求先取得国税局（IRS）的清税证明或缴纳预估税款；加拿大需申报最终税务申报表。税务环节是过户拖期最常见的原因，务必预留时间。

## 第5步：办理产权过户登记（1–3个月）

由当地律师通过产权转让书（Deed of Transfer / Grant Deed）或法院命令，将产权登记到继承人名下，并向当地土地登记机构（如美国的 County Recorder、澳洲的 Land Registry）提交。登记完成后，你才能出售、出租或抵押贷款。

## 时间与费用预期

| 阶段 | 通常周期 | 主要成本 |
|------|---------|---------|
| 文件准备与公证认证 | 1–4周 | 公证/认证费，较低 |
| 遗嘱认证/遗产管理 | 3–12个月 | 法院费用+律师费 |
| 税务申报与清税 | 2–6个月 | 会计师费+税费 |
| 产权过户登记 | 1–3个月 | 律师费+登记费 |

以上为经验区间，实际取决于房产所在州/省、遗产复杂度和是否有争议。遗产总额较小的情况，美国部分州有简化程序（Small Estate Procedure），可明显缩短周期、降低成本。我们不对任何案件结果作出承诺，具体费用与周期以当地法律和评估为准。

## 风险提示

- 不要以为有了中国继承权公证就能直接过户——在普通法国家，当地法院程序不可省略；
- 不要长期空置房产不处理——物业费、地税滞纳金累积，个别地区甚至可能进入税务拍卖程序；
- 不要忽略税务申报——逾期申报会产生罚款和利息，且可能影响产权过户；
- 多位继承人意见不一致会大幅拖延程序，建议尽早书面约定处置方案。

# Inheriting Overseas Property: US, Canada, Australia

A parent or spouse left a property abroad — an apartment in Los Angeles, a townhouse in Vancouver, a house in Sydney. The person has passed away, and the heirs in China cannot even get through the door: without documents from the local court, banks, property managers, and land registries do not recognize you. The property sits vacant while fees keep accruing, and in some states unpaid property taxes can even trigger a tax sale. Many people assume the house automatically passes to the heirs on death. In common law countries, that is not how it works: without probate, the title simply does not transfer to you. The longer you wait, the more it costs.

## Step 1: Gather identity documents and the will (1–4 weeks)

Get your paperwork in order first:

- Death certificate (issued locally; an apostille is usually needed for use in China);
- The original will, if any — where it was made and whether it has been through probate;
- Proof of relationship to the deceased (marriage certificate, birth certificates, household register, etc.).

## Step 2: Open probate or estate administration (3–12 months)

- With a will: the executor applies to the local court for probate; the court confirms the will and grants authority to administer;
- Without a will: the court appoints an administrator, and the estate is distributed under local intestacy rules;
- This step must happen in the court where the property is located. A Chinese notarial certificate of inheritance generally cannot replace the local court process.

## Step 3: Engage local counsel and tax advisors (parallel with Step 2)

Cross-border property inheritance almost always requires local lawyers and accountants: counsel handles the court process and the transfer; accountants handle estate tax, capital gains tax, and transfer tax. The rules differ sharply by country:

- **United States**: federal estate tax applies to non-residents above an exemption (roughly US$13 million for 2026 — verify the current figure), and some states add their own estate or inheritance tax;
- **Canada**: no estate tax, but death is treated as a deemed disposition at fair market value, which can trigger capital gains tax;
- **Australia**: no estate tax, but selling an inherited property may trigger capital gains tax.

Confirm all figures and rules with local professionals for the current year.

## Step 4: Pay taxes and obtain clearance (2–6 months)

Requirements vary: some jurisdictions require estate tax to be paid before transfer; others allow installments or deferral. In the US, heirs typically need an IRS closing letter or an estimated payment; in Canada, a final tax return must be filed. Tax delays are the most common reason transfers stall, so budget time for this step.

## Step 5: Register the transfer (1–3 months)

Local counsel files a deed of transfer (or a court order) with the land registry — for example, the county recorder in the US or the Land Registry in Australia. Only after registration can you sell, rent, or borrow against the property.

## Time and cost expectations

| Stage | Typical duration | Main costs |
|-------|------------------|------------|
| Documents and notarization | 1–4 weeks | Notary/apostille fees, low |
| Probate / administration | 3–12 months | Court fees + counsel fees |
| Tax filing and clearance | 2–6 months | Accountant fees + taxes |
| Title transfer | 1–3 months | Counsel fees + registration |

These are experience ranges; actual figures depend on the state or province, the complexity of the estate, and whether it is contested. For smaller estates, some US states offer simplified procedures (small estate procedures) that significantly shorten the timeline and reduce cost. We make no promise of results for any case; fees and timelines are subject to local law and assessment.

## Key risks

- Do not assume a Chinese inheritance certificate is enough — in common law countries the local court process cannot be skipped;
- Do not leave the property vacant indefinitely — fees and arrears accumulate, and in some regions the tax authority may sell the property;
- Do not ignore tax filings — late filings attract penalties and interest and can block the transfer;
- Disagreements among heirs slow everything down; agree on a plan in writing early.
