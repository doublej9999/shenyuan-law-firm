# 国际律师事务所怎么选：跨境商业纠纷委托指南

一家中国企业发现海外客户拖欠尾款，或者供应商在境外违约时，最容易犯的错误是先在网上寻找“最有名”的国际律师事务所，再把全部材料一次性发过去。没有先确认法域、合同争议解决条款和资产位置，可能导致错误立案、重复支付当地律师费，甚至错过财产保全和诉讼时效窗口。跨境案件的成本往往不是从起诉费开始，而是从错误判断开始累积。

## 第一步：先确定案件真正需要覆盖的法域

把案件拆成三个地点：合同签署或履行地、被告或债务人所在地、主要资产所在地。三者可能不同，适用的法院、仲裁机构、送达方式和执行规则也会不同。国际律师事务所是否在这些法域拥有自有团队并不是唯一标准，更重要的是它能否明确说明：哪一地律师负责主导，哪一地律师负责当地程序，以及谁对最终策略负责。

同时检查合同中的管辖、仲裁和法律适用条款。中国法可以解释合同成立和履行问题，但外国法院的程序、证据规则和执行措施应由当地有资质的律师依据当地法处理，不能把中国法律意见直接当作外国程序意见。

## 第二步：用书面问题筛选团队，而不是只看品牌

建议在首次会议前准备一页案件摘要，并向候选团队提出以下问题：

1. 团队是否处理过与本案相同法域和争议解决机制的案件？要求说明工作类型，不要求或接受虚构的结果承诺。
2. 谁负责合同分析、证据整理、当地律师协调、谈判和出庭？要求列出姓名、执业资格和分工。
3. 如果对方转移资产，团队能否在法律允许的范围内评估冻结令、禁令或其他保全措施？申请条件和证据门槛是什么？
4. 预估费用按小时、阶段还是混合计费？翻译、差旅、专家、法院费和当地合作律师费是否另计？

真正有价值的国际律师事务所应当能把不确定性写清楚，而不是用“全球资源”“百分百追回”等口号替代分析。

## 第三步：建立跨境证据包

先保留合同及全部版本、订单、发票、提单、验货记录、付款流水、邮件和聊天原始载体。对关键电子证据记录导出时间、账号、设备和文件哈希；不要只提交截屏。把中文材料按日期建立索引，并制作英文事实时间线，标出每一笔金额、交付节点和违约通知。

向国际律师事务所说明证据的取得方式。未经授权登录账户、诱导他人提供受保护资料、擅自录音或购买来源不明的个人信息，都可能损害证据可采性并引发合规风险。是否需要公证、认证、海牙附加证明书或宣誓翻译，要由受理地程序律师确认。

## 第四步：让委托书锁定决策和费用边界

委托文件应写明服务范围、适用法域、报告频率、授权上限、利益冲突检查、保密义务、资料保存和退出机制。对阶段性事项设置书面预算，例如法律评估、催款谈判、保全申请、仲裁或诉讼、判决执行分别报价。要求团队在预计超预算前取得书面批准，并约定谁有权代表企业接受和解条件。

如果由中国律师事务所作为总协调方，应明确其与外国合作律师之间是转委托、联合服务还是信息转介。中国律师可以负责中国法和整体协调，但外国法结论及当地出庭资格以当地法律和专业意见为准。

## 时间与费用预期（经验区间）

| 环节 | 经验区间 | 主要费用因素 |
|---|---|---|
| 初步冲突检查与案件评估 | 3天至2周 | 材料数量、法域数量 |
| 证据整理与催收谈判 | 2周至2个月 | 翻译、会议、当地律师参与 |
| 保全、仲裁或诉讼 | 通常3-18个月，视法域而定 | 争议金额、程序和庭次 |
| 判决或裁决执行 | 通常数月至数年，视资产和法域而定 | 执行律师、法院费、调查费 |

以上均为经验区间，不是报价或结果预测。**我们不承诺结果**，实际周期和费用须以案件材料、当地法律及正式委托文件为准。

## 风险提示

- 不要以“国际”标签替代对具体法域执业资格的核验。
- 不要隐瞒已经发出的律师函、和解谈判或其他诉讼程序，避免策略冲突。
- 不要把外国律师的程序建议理解为中国法意见，也不要把中国律师意见直接用于外国法院。
- 对包赢、包追回、先交大额保证金等承诺保持警惕。

跨境商业纠纷需要先判断法域、证据和可执行资产，再决定是否委托国际律师事务所。你可以提交合同、付款和沟通材料，由律师先梳理争议解决路径。

# How to Choose an International Law Firm for a Cross-Border Dispute

A Chinese company may discover that an overseas buyer has stopped paying, or that a foreign supplier has breached its contract. A common mistake is to search for the most famous international law firm and send everything over before checking jurisdiction, dispute-resolution clauses, and the location of assets. That can lead to the wrong filing, duplicate local counsel fees, or missed limitation and preservation windows. Cross-border costs often start with an incorrect assessment, not with the court fee.

## Step 1: Identify the jurisdictions that actually matter

Break the matter into three places: where the contract was signed or performed, where the defendant or debtor is located, and where the important assets are held. They may be different. Each can affect the court, arbitration institution, service, evidence, and enforcement strategy. A firm’s global offices are less important than whether it can state clearly which lawyer leads the matter, which local counsel handles local procedure, and who owns the overall strategy.

Review the jurisdiction, arbitration, and governing-law clauses first. Chinese law may be relevant to contract formation or performance, but foreign procedure, evidence, and remedies must be assessed by a lawyer qualified in that jurisdiction. A Chinese-law opinion is not automatically foreign procedural advice.

## Step 2: Test the team with written questions

Prepare a one-page case summary and ask each candidate:

1. Has the team handled the same jurisdiction and dispute mechanism? Ask about the type of work, not invented outcomes or testimonials.
2. Who handles contract analysis, evidence, local counsel, negotiation, and advocacy? Request names, qualifications, and roles.
3. If assets may be moved, can the team assess freezing orders, injunctions, or other lawful preservation tools? What evidence threshold applies?
4. Are fees hourly, staged, or mixed? Are translation, experts, travel, court fees, and local counsel separate?

A useful international law firm explains uncertainty in writing. Phrases such as “global resources” or “guaranteed recovery” are not a substitute for analysis.

## Step 3: Build a cross-border evidence package

Preserve the contract and all versions, orders, invoices, bills of lading, inspection records, payment records, emails, and original chat exports. For key electronic evidence, record the export time, account, device, and file hash; screenshots alone may be insufficient. Create a dated index and an English fact chronology identifying each amount, delivery milestone, and notice of breach.

Tell counsel how each item was obtained. Unauthorised account access, inducement, unlawful recording, or purchasing dubious personal data can affect admissibility and create compliance exposure. The receiving court or tribunal should determine whether notarisation, legalisation, an apostille, or sworn translation is required.

## Step 4: Make the engagement letter control scope and cost

The engagement should state scope, jurisdictions, reporting frequency, authority limits, conflicts checks, confidentiality, document retention, and termination rights. Set written budgets for evaluation, demand and negotiation, preservation, arbitration or litigation, and enforcement. Require approval before a material overrun and identify who may accept settlement terms for the company.

If a Chinese firm coordinates foreign counsel, clarify whether the relationship is a subcontract, joint service, or referral. The Chinese lawyer may handle PRC law and coordination, while foreign-law advice and local advocacy depend on local law and professional advice.

## Time and cost expectations (experience ranges)

| Stage | Experience range | Main cost drivers |
|---|---|---|
| Conflicts check and initial assessment | 3 days to 2 weeks | Volume and number of jurisdictions |
| Evidence review and demand negotiation | 2 weeks to 2 months | Translation, meetings, local counsel |
| Preservation, arbitration, or litigation | Usually 3–18 months, by jurisdiction | Value, procedure, hearings |
| Judgment or award enforcement | Usually months to years, by jurisdiction and assets | Counsel, court, investigation costs |

These are experience ranges, not a quote or prediction. **We do not promise results**. The actual timeline and fees depend on the documents, local law, and signed engagement.

## Risk warnings

- Do not treat the word “international” as proof of local licensing.
- Disclose prior demand letters, settlement talks, and proceedings to prevent strategy conflicts.
- Do not treat foreign procedural advice as PRC-law advice, or vice versa.
- Be cautious of guaranteed wins, guaranteed recovery, or large “security deposits.”
