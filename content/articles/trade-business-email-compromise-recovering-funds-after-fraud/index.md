# 收到「假客户」邮件被骗定金，钱能追回吗？

你正谈着一笔外贸订单，客户"临时"发来邮件，说收款账户有变动，让你把定金转到一个新账户；你核对抬头确实是客户公司的名字，钱转过去了，等真客户来催款你才发现——那封邮件是假的，钱早被转走。外贸邮件诈骗（Business Email Compromise, BEC）几乎天天都在发生，骗走的货款和定金往往流向境外账户。这不是认栽的题，而是一场和时间的赛跑：第一时间取证、通知银行止付、向境外追查冻结，才有机会把钱追回来。拖得越久，资金被层层转移的风险越大。

## 第一步：先"止血"——立即止付与通知银行

- **第一时间联系收款银行/汇款银行**：要求撤回或冻结转账，越快越可能阻断；\\
- **通知开户行与当地反诈机制**：启动紧急止付与协查流程；\\
- **保存全部邮件与转账凭证**：含伪造邮件的完整头信息、收款账户信息，作为追查证据。

## 第二步：同步固定证据、梳理资金流向

把伪造邮件、聊天记录、合同、定金付款凭证，以及对方宣称的"账户变更"往来全部保存，并整理出资金被转到的具体账户与银行。证据是否完整，直接影响后续能否立案、止付和追查，务必第一时间备份并做好时间戳。

## 第三步：多渠道追查与冻结

- **刑事报案**：向被骗资金往来涉及地报案，争取立案并启动涉案资金止付/返还机制；\\
- **向收款账户所在地追查**：联合当地律师，对疑似洗钱账户申请冻结与调查；\\
- **申请民事保全/冻结令**：在可能的情况下申请对目标账户的临时保全，防止资金转移。

## 第四步：评估追回路径与可执行性

追回能否成功，取决于报警及时性、资金流向是否清晰、各法域能否配合，以及收款账户是否还有钱。跨境追查可能涉及侦查协作与司法协助，路径往往比境内诈骗更长。决定投入前，先请律师对追回可行性做评估，再决定刑事、民事还是并行推进。

## 时间与费用预期

| 环节 | 经验周期 | 主要成本 |
|------|---------|---------|
| 止付与初步联系 | 数小时至数天 | 银行/协查费用(视环节) |
| 证据固定与报案 | 数天至数周 | 公证/翻译等证据费用 |
| 跨境追查与冻结 | 数周至数月，视法域 | 律师费+司法协助费用 |
| 追回与返还 | 视侦查与司法协作进度 | 律师费+可能的分成安排 |

以上均为经验区间，取决于金额、资金流向与法域配合度。**我们不承诺结果**，但第一时间止付和固定证据，是提高追回可能性的关键。

## 风险提示

- 伪造邮件常伪装成主体变更，务必通过电话、视频等二次确认收款信息再转账；\\
- 别等资金被多层转移才动手，止付和冻结的窗口通常很短；\\
- 跨境追查涉及多法域与司法协助，以当地法律与专业意见为准；\\
- 谨防"包追回"的承诺，理性评估成本与可能性。

# Business Email Compromise: Recovering Funds After Fraud

You are closing a trade order when the "customer" emails a last-minute change: the payee account has changed, so wire the deposit to a new account. The e-mail header matches the customer's name, you send the money—and then the real customer asks where it is. That e-mail was forged and the funds are gone, often into an overseas account. Business Email Compromise (BEC) happens every day. This is not a case to write off; it is a race against time. The first step, notifying the bank, and tracing and freezing funds abroad are the only way to stand a chance of recovery. The longer you wait, the more the money moves on and the harder it gets.

## Step 1: stop the loss first—freeze and notify the bank

- **Contact the receiving/remitting bank immediately**: request a recall or freeze; the faster you act, the more likely you are to stop the transfer;\\
- **Notify your bank and local anti-fraud channels**: activate emergency stop-payment and cooperation procedures;\\
- **Save all e-mails and transfer records**: keep the full headers of the forged e-mail and the receiving account details as evidence.

## Step 2: fix the evidence and map the money flow

Back up all forged e-mails, chats, contracts, the deposit payment record, and the "account change" correspondence, and record the exact account and bank the funds went to. Complete evidence directly affects whether the matter can be filed, stopped, and traced—back it up immediately and note the timestamps.

## Step 3: trace and freeze through multiple channels

- **File a criminal complaint**: report to the jurisdictions involved and seek filing and stop-payment/return procedures for the funds;\\
- **Trace the receiving account**: work with local counsel to apply for freezing and investigation of suspected money-laundering accounts;\\
- **Apply for civil preservation/freezing orders**: where possible, obtain provisional preservation of the target account to stop further transfers.

## Step 4: assess the recovery path and enforceability

Whether you recover depends on how quickly you reported, how clear the money flow is, whether jurisdictions cooperate, and whether the receiving account still holds funds. Cross-border tracing may involve mutual legal assistance and often takes longer than domestic fraud. Before committing resources, have a lawyer assess feasibility, then decide whether to pursue criminal, civil, or parallel routes.

## Time and cost expectations

| Stage | Typical timeline | Main costs |
|-------|------------------|------------|
| Stop payment and initial contact | Hours to days | Bank/cooperation fees (as applicable) |
| Evidence fixing and filing | Days to weeks | Notarisation/translation costs |
| Cross-border tracing and freezing | Weeks to months, by jurisdiction | Legal fees + mutual legal assistance costs |

These are experience ranges; figures depend on the amount, the money flow, and jurisdictional cooperation. **We do not promise results**, but acting on stop-payment and evidence immediately is the key to improving the odds.

## Key risks

- Forged e-mails often mimic a change of details—always re-validate payment instructions by phone or video before wiring;\\
- Do not wait until the funds have moved through several layers—the freeze window is usually short;\\
- Cross-border tracing involves multiple jurisdictions and mutual legal assistance—follow local law and professional advice;\\
- Be wary of "guaranteed recovery" promises and weigh costs realistically.
