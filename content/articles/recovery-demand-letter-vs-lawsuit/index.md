# 律师函 vs 诉讼：哪种方式更适合你的欠款？

欠款追收，很多人一上来就问"要不要起诉"。其实，起诉是工具箱里最重的工具，不是唯一工具，也不一定是最优工具。在律师函和诉讼之间做选择，核心看四个变量：金额、证据、债务人偿付能力、时效压力。

## 先算一笔账：两种方式的成本对比

| 维度 | 律师函 | 诉讼/仲裁 |
|------|--------|----------|
| 周期 | 1–2周 | 3–12个月+执行 |
| 成本 | 固定费用，通常数千元量级 | 律师费+程序费+保全费，数万起步 |
| 威慑力 | 中等，表明法律程序启动意愿 | 强，具有强制执行力 |
| 风险 | 低，失败无损 | 中高，败诉承担费用与时间 |
| 适用 | 对方有还款能力但拖延/态度不明 | 对方失联、赖账、明显无协商意愿 |

经验上，**金额不大、证据齐全、债务人仍在经营**的案件，律师函的成功率并不低；而**金额大、债务人失联或转移资产**的案件，诉讼（配合财产保全）才是正解。

## 律师函适合的场景

1. **债务人只是资金紧张**：收到正式律师函后，很多企业主会选择分期解决——他们怕的是"事情闹大"；
2. **双方还有合作可能**：不希望撕破脸，但需要施压；
3. **证据有瑕疵**：先用律师函"套"出对方对欠款的书面确认，为后续诉讼固定证据；
4. **时效即将到期**：律师函是中断时效的低成本手段；
5. **小额欠款**：诉讼成本可能超过欠款本身，律师函+协商更划算。

## 诉讼/仲裁适合的场景

1. **债务人失联或明确赖账**：协商窗口已关闭；
2. **金额大**：值得投入诉讼成本；
3. **需要财产保全**：只有启动法律程序才能申请冻结账户、查封资产；
4. **涉及跨境执行**：需要判决/裁决作为执行依据；
5. **债务人有转移资产迹象**：越早保全越有利。

## 一个容易忽略的问题：诉讼时效

无论选择哪种方式，先确认时效。如果欠款已经超过诉讼时效且无中断事由，诉讼可能面临败诉风险，此时律师函和协商是更现实的选择。时效计算因法域而异，拿不准就尽快咨询。

## 第三条路：先律师函，后诉讼

实务中最常见的组合是：**先发律师函（给7–15天），到期不解决再启动诉讼/仲裁**。这样做有三个好处：

- 律师函中断时效，诉讼不慌；
- 给对方一次体面解决的机会，避免无谓诉讼；
- 如果对方在律师函后书面承认欠款，后续诉讼的证据将非常扎实。

## 决策建议

- 金额 < 律师费预期的 2–3 倍：优先律师函+协商；
- 金额大 + 对方正常经营：律师函，同时准备诉讼；
- 金额大 + 失联/转移资产：直接诉讼/仲裁 + 财产保全，不要浪费窗口期。

# Demand Letter vs Lawsuit: Which Works for Your Debt?

When a debt goes unpaid, many people immediately ask "should I sue?" But a lawsuit is the heaviest tool in the box — not the only one, and not always the best. Choosing between a demand letter and a lawsuit comes down to four variables: the amount, the evidence, the debtor's ability to pay, and limitation pressure.

## First, the cost math

| Dimension | Demand letter | Lawsuit / arbitration |
|-----------|---------------|-----------------------|
| Timeline | 1–2 weeks | 3–12 months + enforcement |
| Cost | Fixed fee, low | Counsel + court/tribunal + preservation fees, significant |
| Leverage | Moderate — signals intent to proceed | Strong — backed by enforcement power |
| Risk | Low; no downside if it fails | Medium-high; costs and time if you lose |
| Best for | Capable but delaying debtor | Missing, defiant, or non-responsive debtor |

Experience suggests that **small-to-medium debts with solid evidence and an operating debtor** respond well to demand letters; **large debts, disappearing debtors, or asset movement** call for proceedings with preservation.

## When a demand letter fits

1. The debtor is cash-tight but operating — many owners settle to avoid escalation;
2. You want to preserve the business relationship while applying pressure;
3. Your evidence has gaps — the letter may draw out a written acknowledgment of the debt;
4. The limitation period is near — a letter is a cheap way to interrupt it;
5. Small debts — where suing costs more than the debt.

## When proceedings fit

1. The debtor has disappeared or openly refuses to pay;
2. The amount justifies the cost;
3. You need asset preservation — freezing accounts and seizing assets requires proceedings;
4. Cross-border enforcement is involved — you need a judgment or award to enforce;
5. The debtor shows signs of moving assets — the earlier the preservation, the better.

## The variable people overlook: the limitation period

Whichever path you choose, check the limitation period first. If the debt is time-barred with no interrupting event, litigation may fail — making the letter and negotiation the realistic route. Limitation rules vary by jurisdiction; when in doubt, ask early.

## The third path: letter first, then sue

The most common sequence in practice: **send a demand letter (7–15 days), and file proceedings only if it fails.** Three benefits:

- The letter interrupts the limitation period, so suing later is safe;
- It gives the debtor a dignified way out, avoiding unnecessary litigation;
- If the debtor acknowledges the debt in writing after the letter, your evidence becomes much stronger.

## Decision guide

- Amount below 2–3× expected legal cost: letter + negotiation first;
- Large amount + operating debtor: letter, while preparing proceedings;
- Large amount + missing debtor or asset movement: file directly with preservation — do not waste the window.
