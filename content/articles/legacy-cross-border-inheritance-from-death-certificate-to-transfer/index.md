# 跨境继承的基本流程：从死亡证明到遗产过户

人在海外去世，遗产却分布在两个甚至三个国家——这是跨境继承最典型的场景，也是最容易让人无从下手的场景。很多继承人第一步就卡住：不知道该先办死亡证明的翻译公证，还是先找律师，还是先查银行账户。每一步都有严格的先后顺序，走错一步就可能多花几个月。本文把整个流程拆成六个环节，帮您建立一张"继承路线图"。

## 环节一：取得并认证死亡证明

- 从死亡地政府机构取得死亡证明（Death Certificate）；
- 海外文件用于中国境内程序时，通常需办理海牙认证（Apostille）或领事认证；反之，中国文件用于海外程序也需要相应认证；
- 同时准备翻译件，多数机构要求经认证的翻译。

## 环节二：确定适用法律与遗嘱效力

- 先判断继承适用哪国法律：一般涉及被继承人死亡时经常居所地法、国籍国法或不动产所在地法，具体以各国冲突法规则为准；
- 有遗嘱的，确认遗嘱在相关国家是否有效；多份遗嘱可能在不同国家分别被认定，务必逐一处理；
- 无遗嘱的，按各遗产所在地的法定继承规则确定继承人范围。

## 环节三：确认继承人并固定权利

- 准备亲属关系证明、身份证明、婚姻状况证明等材料，均需公证认证；
- 继承人之间无争议的，可办理继承权公证或取得遗产管理文书；有争议的，可能需要诉讼或调解；
- 海外继承人通常可以委托当地与国内律师协同办理，本人可不必全程到场。

## 环节四：清点遗产与处理债务

- 全面盘点遗产：银行账户、房产、股权、保险、养老金、加密货币等，一处都不能漏；
- 查询遗产所在国的债务与税务情况：遗产税、资本利得税、未偿债务会影响实际可分得的金额；
- 决定是否接受继承：债务超过资产时，部分法域允许放弃继承或选择"限定继承"，需在法定期限内作出。

## 环节五：办理过户与资金归集

- 房产：在不动产所在地办理产权转移登记；股权：按公司章程与登记机关要求办理变更；
- 金融资产：凭遗产管理文书/继承权证明向银行、券商申请划转或变现；
- 保险金：确认受益人条款——指定受益人的保险金通常不进入遗产，直接赔付。

## 环节六：跨境资金汇回与税务申报

- 资金跨境转移需遵守外汇管理与中国税务申报要求，大额资金建议提前规划合规路径；
- 部分国家有遗产税申报义务，未申报可能产生罚息；中国目前未开征遗产税，但境外所得涉及个人所得税申报的，以主管税务机关要求为准。

## 时间与费用预期

| 环节 | 通常周期 | 成本量级 |
|------|---------|---------|
| 死亡证明取得与认证 | 2–8 周 | 低 |
| 遗嘱认定/法律适用分析 | 1–3 个月 | 中 |
| 继承人确认（公证/诉讼） | 1–6 个月 | 中高 |
| 遗产清点与税务评估 | 1–3 个月 | 中 |
| 过户与资金归集 | 3–12 个月 | 中高 |
| 资金汇回与申报 | 1–6 个月 | 低中 |

以上为经验区间，实际以遗产规模、分布国家数与争议情况为准。

## 风险提示

- 不要先花大钱再办事：程序顺序错了，前面做的公证认证可能全部作废重来；
- 不要漏报资产：漏掉的账户和房产可能因长期无人认领被收归国有或产生高额费用；
- 不要忽视债务与税务：先评估"净遗产"再决定是否接受，避免继承一堆负债；
- 不要自己翻译认证文件：认证翻译有特定格式要求，出错会被退回。

# Cross-Border Inheritance: From Death Certificate to Transfer

A relative dies abroad, and the estate is scattered across two or even three countries — the classic cross-border inheritance scenario, and the one that leaves heirs frozen at step one. Should they translate the death certificate first, hire a lawyer, or check the bank accounts? Every step has a strict order, and one wrong move can cost months. This article breaks the whole process into six stages so you can build an inheritance roadmap.

## Stage 1: Obtain and authenticate the death certificate

- Get the death certificate from the authorities where the death occurred;
- Documents from abroad used in Chinese procedures generally require apostille or consular legalization, and vice versa for Chinese documents used abroad;
- Prepare certified translations — most authorities require authenticated translations.

## Stage 2: Determine the applicable law and will validity

- Identify which law governs the inheritance: usually the deceased's habitual residence, nationality, or the location of immovable property — check each country's conflict rules;
- If there is a will, confirm whether it is valid in each relevant country; multiple wills may be treated differently country by country;
- Without a will, statutory inheritance rules of each asset location determine the heirs.

## Stage 3: Confirm the heirs and fix their rights

- Prepare kinship, identity, and marital status documents, all notarized and authenticated;
- If heirs agree, complete a notarized inheritance confirmation or obtain letters of administration; disagreements may require litigation or mediation;
- Overseas heirs can usually authorize coordinated local and Chinese counsel without traveling for every step.

## Stage 4: Inventory the estate and handle debts

- List everything: bank accounts, real property, shares, insurance, pensions, even crypto — nothing can be skipped;
- Check the estate's debts and taxes in each country: estate tax, capital gains tax, and unpaid debts reduce what heirs actually receive;
- Decide whether to accept: where debts exceed assets, some jurisdictions allow renunciation or acceptance with limited liability — decide within the statutory window.

## Stage 5: Transfer title and consolidate funds

- Real property: register the transfer where the property sits; shares: follow the articles of association and registry procedures;
- Financial assets: use the letters of administration or inheritance certificate to transfer or liquidate accounts with banks and brokers;
- Insurance: check beneficiary clauses — proceeds payable to a named beneficiary usually bypass the estate entirely.

## Stage 6: Repatriate funds and file taxes

- Cross-border transfers must follow foreign exchange controls and Chinese tax reporting rules; plan a compliant path for large amounts;
- Some countries require estate tax filings — failing to file can trigger penalties. China currently has no estate tax, but any foreign income may still be subject to personal income tax reporting as required by the competent tax authority.

## Timeline and cost expectations

| Stage | Typical duration | Cost level |
|-------|------------------|------------|
| Death certificate and authentication | 2–8 weeks | low |
| Will validity / applicable law | 1–3 months | medium |
| Heir confirmation (notarial / litigation) | 1–6 months | medium-high |
| Inventory and tax assessment | 1–3 months | medium |
| Transfer and consolidation | 3–12 months | medium-high |
| Repatriation and filing | 1–6 months | low-medium |

These are experience ranges; actuals depend on the estate size, number of countries, and any disputes.

## Risks to watch

- Do not spend before planning: with the wrong order, completed notarizations and authentications may need to be redone;
- Do not omit assets: overlooked accounts and properties can be escheated to the state or accrue charges over time;
- Do not ignore debts and taxes: assess the net estate before accepting, or you may inherit a pile of liabilities;
- Do not translate documents yourself: authenticated translation has specific format requirements, and errors mean rejection.
