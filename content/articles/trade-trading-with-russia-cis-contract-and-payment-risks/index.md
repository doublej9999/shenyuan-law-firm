# 与俄罗斯/独联体客户交易的合同与收款风险

你的俄罗斯或哈萨克斯坦客户下了大单，可到了结算环节——银行通道换了、汇率一顿波动、对方账户收款受阻，钱迟迟到不了账，货却已经发出去。与俄罗斯及独联体地区客户交易，最大的风险往往不在产品质量，而在**收款与履约的制度性障碍**：制裁与合规限制、本地银行与 SWIFT 通道受限、卢布与当地货币汇率剧烈波动，都可能让你"货发出去了钱收不回来"。很多出口商正是在合同里没把这些风险约定清楚，才在纠纷面前被动挨打。下面给出一条针对该区域的实操路径，帮你把合同与收款设计得更稳。

## 第一步：合同条款先锁定风险

- **明确结算币种与汇率风险归属**：约定以美元、欧元或人民币中的何种货币结算，并约定汇率大幅波动的分担或调整机制，避免卢布贬值吞掉利润；
- **写清付款节点与违约金**：把预付款、发货后、验收后的付款节点与逾期违约金写进合同，尤其要约定"货物所有权保留"或物权保留条款，作为收款保障；
- **加入制裁与不可抗力条款**：明确若因制裁、银行通道中断导致无法付款或发货，双方的权利义务如何调整，减少事后扯皮。

## 第二步：选择更稳妥的结算方式

- **优先信用证（L/C）或银行托收**：较之纯粹的赊销或 T/T，信用证能显著降低对方违约风险，但要确保开证行与你的银行可顺利对接；
- **提高预付款比例**：对资信一般的新客户，尽量提高订金比例，减少垫资风险；
- **谨慎处理第三方代付**：若客户以离岸公司或第三方账户代付，务必核实收款路径与合规性，避免触发制裁或洗钱审查。

## 第三步：收款与履约的日常管理

- **跟踪银行通道与合规要求**：随时留意本地银行与跨境清算通道的可操作性，必要时咨询专业机构关于出口合规的意见；
- **勤对账、早催收**：付款到期即发书面催告，避免因"不好意思"拖过时效；
- **买出口信用/政治风险保险**：对高风险区域，可考虑通过出口信用保险工具对冲收款风险，具体以你的实际情况与当地规则为准。

## 时间与费用预期

| 环节 | 经验周期 | 主要成本 |
|------|---------|---------|
| 合同与结算设计 | 数天至数周 | 低-中（律师/合规） |
| 跨境收付款 | 视通道与银行而定 | 银行/通道手续费 |
| 逾期催收与交涉 | 数周至数月 | 低-中（律师函/协商） |
| 争议仲裁/诉讼 | 6–24 个月 | 律师费+程序费 |

以上均为经验区间，具体取决于金额、客户、结算通道与合规状况——**我们不承诺结果**，但把风险写进合同、选对结算方式，往往是能否收回货款的分水岭。

## 风险提示

- 切忌在合同中漏掉币种、汇率与付款节点约定，事后争议无从依凭；
- 不要忽视制裁与合规红线，违规收款可能带来远超货值的法律后果；
- 不要因为对方"量大"就放松预付款或担保要求；
- 中国法律与俄罗斯/独联体国家法律差异大，拿不准的以当地法律/专业意见为准。

# Trading with Russia & CIS: Contract and Payment Risks

Your Russian or Kazakh buyer has placed a large order, but at settlement the banking channel changes, the currency swings, payments are blocked, and the money never arrives — while the goods are already shipped. With Russia and the CIS, the biggest risk is rarely product quality; it is the **institutional obstacles to payment and performance**: sanctions and compliance limits, restricted local-bank and SWIFT channels, and violent swings in the ruble or local currencies can all leave you "goods shipped, money not collected." Many exporters get squeezed precisely because the contract never addressed these risks. Here is a practical path to make your contract and payment structure more robust.

## Step 1: lock the risks into the contract first

- **Clarify the settlement currency and who bears FX risk**: state whether settlement is in USD, EUR, or CNY, and set how large currency swings are shared or adjusted, so a weak ruble does not erase your margin;
- **Write payment milestones and penalties**: set out advance, post-shipment, and post-inspection triggers plus late-payment penalties, and include a retention-of-title clause as security for payment;
- **Add sanctions and force-majeure clauses**: define rights and duties if sanctions or banking interruptions block payment or delivery, cutting later disputes.

## Step 2: choose more secure settlement methods

- **Prefer letters of credit (L/C) or bank collections**: versus open account or T/T, an L/C materially reduces default risk, but ensure the issuing bank and your bank can work together smoothly;
- **Raise advance-payment ratios**: for less-established new clients, ask for higher deposits to reduce your financing exposure;
- **Treat third-party payments cautiously**: if the client pays via an offshore company or a third-party account, verify the path and compliance to avoid triggering sanctions or anti-money-laundering reviews.

## Step 3: manage collection and performance day to day

- **Track banking channels and compliance**: keep watching whether local banks and cross-border clearing channels remain workable, and consult professionals on export-compliance advice where needed;
- **Reconcile often, chase early**: send a written demand as soon as payment falls due, rather than "politely" letting the limitation period slip;
- **Consider export credit/political-risk insurance**: for high-risk regions, insurance products can hedge collection risk — subject to your actual situation and local rules.

## Time and cost expectations

| Stage | Typical timeline | Main costs |
|-------|------------------|------------|
| Contract and settlement design | Days to weeks | Low–medium (counsel/compliance) |
| Cross-border payment | Depends on channel and banks | Bank/channel fees |
| Overdue collection and negotiation | Weeks to months | Low–medium (demand letters) |
| Dispute arbitration/litigation | 6–24 months | Counsel + procedural fees |

These are experience ranges; actual figures depend on amount, client, settlement channel, and compliance — **we do not promise results**, but building risk into the contract and choosing the right settlement method often decide whether the trade debt is recovered.

## Key risks

- Do not omit currency, FX, and payment-milestone terms in the contract, leaving disputes baseless later;
- Do not ignore sanctions and compliance red lines — non-compliant payments can bring consequences far beyond the value of the goods;
- Do not relax deposit or guarantee requirements just because the volume is "large";
- China law and Russian/CIS law differ; when in doubt, follow local law and professional advice.
