# 中国公民在海外去世，遗产如何处理？

人在海外去世，遗产可能横跨两个甚至多个国家：海外的房产、存款、公司股权，国内的房产、存款、理财产品。对于国内的继承人来说，最难的往往不是法律本身，而是**不知道从哪一步开始**。本文梳理一条可执行的处理路径。

## 第一步：取得死亡与身份文件

- 取得当地出具的死亡证明（Death Certificate），并按当地要求办理认证（海牙认证或领事认证）；
- 确认逝者国籍身份文件（护照、身份证、户口注销证明）与亲属关系证明（结婚证、出生证明、户籍档案）；
- 若涉及国内程序，还需办理相关文件的翻译与公证。

## 第二步：确认继承依据：遗嘱还是法定继承

- **有遗嘱**：查找遗嘱原件并确认其效力。海外遗嘱是否覆盖中国境内遗产、是否需要当地遗嘱认证（Probate），都要逐项确认；
- **无遗嘱**：按遗产所在地的法定继承规则处理——**关键是：各国法定继承顺序和份额不同，先厘清适用哪国法律**（通常涉及"属人法"与"遗产所在地法"的协调，也可能存在冲突规则）。

这一步的结论直接决定后续所有动作，建议在专业协助下完成。

## 第三步：盘点遗产清单

- 海外资产：房产、银行存款、投资账户、保险、公司股权、车辆等；
- 国内资产：房产、存款、证券、企业股权等；
- 负债：贷款、抵押、税款——遗产可能资不抵债，继承人需要评估是否接受继承。

遗产清单不仅是办理继承的基础，也是判断"先处理哪国"的依据。

## 第四步：办理认证与继承程序

- 海外资产：通常需在当地启动遗嘱认证/遗产管理程序，由当地法院指定的遗产管理人或遗嘱执行人处理资产清算与分配；国内继承人需配合提供公证文件与授权文件；
- 国内资产：在境内办理继承公证或诉讼确认继承权，再办理过户/提取；
- **顺序建议**：先确认适用法律与程序，再启动具体程序，避免"办了一半发现文件不对"。

## 第五步：跨境转移与税务

- 继承款汇回中国：需按外汇管理规定办理，保留完整的继承与完税文件；
- 税务：部分国家征收遗产税/继承税（如美国、英国、部分欧洲国家），需在遗产分配前评估税务义务并完成申报；中国目前未开征遗产税，但继承收入的相关合规仍需注意；
- 全球资产申报：如涉及CRS/FATCA等申报义务，需评估历史申报状态与补救可能。

## 周期与成本参考

| 事项 | 通常周期 |
|------|---------|
| 文件公证认证 | 1–3个月 |
| 遗嘱认证/遗产管理 | 6–24个月（视法域与遗产复杂程度） |
| 国内继承办理 | 3–12个月 |
| 资金跨境转移 | 1–3个月（视金额与文件） |

## 给继承人的建议

1. **先确认法律依据，再动程序**：各国继承规则差异大，先厘清适用法律与程序顺序；
2. **文件一次备齐**：死亡证明、亲属关系、遗嘱原件、资产凭证，尽量一次性公证认证，减少往返成本；
3. **评估债务与税务**：遗产可能含债务，先评估再决定是否接受继承；
4. **尽早启动**：部分国家对遗产处理有时间限制，拖延可能增加复杂性与成本。

# Chinese Citizen Dies Abroad: Handling the Estate

When someone dies abroad, the estate may span two or more countries: property, bank deposits, and equity overseas, plus property, deposits, and financial products in China. For heirs in China, the hardest part is usually not the law itself — it is **not knowing where to begin**. This article sets out a workable path.

## Step 1: Obtain death and identity documents

- Obtain the local death certificate and have it legalized as required (apostille or consular legalization);
- Confirm the deceased's identity documents (passport, ID, household cancellation) and relationship proof (marriage certificate, birth certificates, household records);
- For Chinese procedures, arrange translation and notarization of relevant documents.

## Step 2: Confirm the basis of inheritance: will or intestacy

- **With a will**: locate the original and verify its validity. Confirm whether the will covers China-situated assets and whether local probate is required;
- **Without a will**: follow the intestacy rules of the asset's location — **critically, intestacy orders and shares differ by country, so first determine which law applies** (typically coordinating personal law and the lex situs, possibly under conflict rules).

This conclusion drives everything that follows — it is worth doing with professional assistance.

## Step 3: Inventory the estate

- Overseas assets: property, bank deposits, investment accounts, insurance, company equity, vehicles;
- China assets: property, deposits, securities, business equity;
- Liabilities: loans, mortgages, taxes — an estate may be insolvent, so heirs must assess whether to accept.

The inventory is not only the basis for inheritance; it determines which country to handle first.

## Step 4: Legalization and inheritance procedures

- Overseas assets: typically require local probate or estate administration, with assets liquidated and distributed by a court-appointed administrator or executor; heirs in China must provide notarized documents and authorizations;
- China assets: obtain an inheritance certificate (notarized or via court confirmation), then transfer or withdraw;
- **Suggested order**: confirm the applicable law and procedure first, then start — avoid "halfway through and the documents are wrong."

## Step 5: Cross-border transfer and tax

- Transferring inherited funds to China: comply with foreign-exchange rules and keep complete inheritance and tax documentation;
- Tax: some countries levy estate or inheritance taxes (e.g. the US, the UK, parts of Europe) — assess and file before distribution; China currently has no estate tax, but related compliance still matters;
- Global asset reporting: if CRS/FATCA obligations are in play, assess historical filing status and remediation options.

## Timeline and cost reference

| Item | Typical duration |
|------|------------------|
| Document notarization / legalization | 1–3 months |
| Probate / estate administration | 6–24 months (jurisdiction and complexity dependent) |
| China inheritance procedure | 3–12 months |
| Cross-border fund transfer | 1–3 months (amount and documents dependent) |

## Advice for heirs

1. **Confirm the legal basis before starting**: inheritance rules differ sharply by country — clarify applicable law and procedure order first;
2. **Prepare documents in one pass**: death certificate, relationship proof, will, asset vouchers — notarize and legalize at once to cut round-trips;
3. **Assess debts and tax**: estates may carry liabilities; evaluate before deciding whether to accept;
4. **Start early**: some countries impose time limits on estate matters; delay adds complexity and cost.
