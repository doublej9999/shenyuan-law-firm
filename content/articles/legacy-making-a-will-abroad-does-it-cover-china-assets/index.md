# 在海外立遗嘱，能覆盖中国境内的遗产吗？

长期定居海外，在中国还有一套房、一笔存款。几年前在居住国请律师立了遗嘱，把所有财产都留给配偶和子女。问题来了：这份海外遗嘱，管不管中国境内的资产？很多人想当然地认为"遗嘱就是遗嘱"，直到办理继承时才被告知：文件不被认可、程序要走两遍、甚至资产归属与遗嘱意愿相反。

## 一、先看遗嘱的形式能否被中国承认

中国《民法典》规定了自书、代书、打印、录音录像、口头（危急情况下）和公证遗嘱等形式。海外遗嘱能否在中国境内生效，核心是判断适用哪国法律认定其"形式与实质效力"——这属于国际私法问题，通常要看遗嘱人经常居所地、国籍国、遗产所在地等连接点，具体以《中华人民共和国涉外民事关系法律适用法》及相关司法解释为准。实践中，经公证并办理领事认证或附加证明书（Apostille）的海外遗嘱，被国内承认的可能性更高。

## 二、再看遗嘱的内容能否执行

- **必留份**：中国法下，遗嘱不得剥夺缺乏劳动能力又没有生活来源的继承人的必要遗产份额；若海外遗嘱完全忽略这类继承人，涉及中国遗产的部分可能被认定无效或调整；
- **夫妻共同财产**：遗嘱只能处分自己的份额，配偶的一半不受遗嘱约束；
- **不动产**：不动产继承适用不动产所在地法律——中国境内的房产，其继承规则以中国法为准，海外遗嘱不能"绕开"中国法。

## 三、程序上要过几道关

1. 将海外遗嘱原件办理公证、规范的中文翻译，并完成领事认证或 Apostille；
2. 向中国境内公证机构申请办理继承权公证，或向法院提起继承纠纷诉讼，由机构对遗嘱效力作出认定；
3. 凭认定文件办理房产过户、存款支取等手续。

## 四、实务建议

- "双轨并行"：海外遗嘱之外，对中国境内资产单独订立遗嘱，并注明与海外遗嘱的关系；
- 两地遗嘱的受益安排保持一致，避免冲突；
- 定期更新——婚姻、子女、国籍等变化都可能影响遗嘱效力。

## 时间与费用预期

| 环节 | 通常周期 | 主要成本 |
|------|---------|---------|
| 公证/认证/Apostille | 2–8 周 | 公证费+认证费+翻译费 |
| 继承权公证/诉讼 | 1–6 个月 | 公证费或诉讼费+律师费 |
| 过户/支取 | 1–3 个月 | 税费+手续费 |

## 风险提示

- 不要以为"遗嘱全球通用"——效力认定按各国法律分别进行；
- 不要遗漏必留份与夫妻共同财产问题，否则遗嘱可能部分无效；
- 不要忽略中国境内的单独程序——海外遗嘱通常不能直接用于国内过户；

# Making a Will Abroad: Does It Cover China Assets?

You have lived abroad for years, but you still own an apartment and bank deposits in China. Years ago you had a lawyer in your country of residence draft a will leaving everything to your spouse and children. The question is: does that foreign will actually govern your Chinese assets? Many people assume "a will is a will" — until the inheritance process reveals that the document is not recognized, the procedure must be run twice, or the assets end up somewhere the testator never intended.

## First: will the form of the foreign will be recognized in China?

China's Civil Code recognizes several will forms: holographic (handwritten), attested, printed, audio/video recorded, notarial, and oral (in emergencies). Whether a foreign will takes effect in China depends on which law governs its formal and substantive validity — a conflict-of-laws question resolved by reference to connecting factors such as the testator's habitual residence, nationality, and the location of the assets, under China's Law on the Application of Laws to Foreign-Related Civil Relations and relevant judicial interpretations. In practice, a foreign will that is notarized and carries consular legalization or an Apostille has a better chance of being accepted.

## Second: will the content be enforceable?

- **Compulsory shares**: Chinese law protects heirs who lack the ability to work and have no other source of livelihood; a will that entirely disinherits such persons may be invalid or adjusted as to the Chinese assets;
- **Marital property**: a will can only dispose of the testator's own share — the spouse's half is not affected;
- **Real estate**: succession to immovable property follows the law of the place where the property is located. For real estate in China, Chinese rules apply; a foreign will cannot bypass them.

## Third: the procedural steps

1. Notarize the original foreign will, obtain a proper Chinese translation, and complete consular legalization or Apostille;
2. Apply to a Chinese notary office for a certificate of inheritance, or bring a succession action in court, for a determination of the will's validity;
3. Use that determination to transfer property and withdraw deposits.

## Practical suggestions

- Run two tracks: a foreign will plus a separate Chinese will for China-based assets, stating how the two relate;
- Keep the beneficiary arrangements consistent across both to avoid conflicts;
- Review and update both wills when marriage, children, or nationality changes.

## Time and cost expectations

| Step | Typical duration | Main costs |
|------|------------------|------------|
| Notarization / legalization / Apostille | 2–8 weeks | Notary + legalization + translation fees |
| Certificate of inheritance / litigation | 1–6 months | Notary or court fees + counsel fees |
| Transfer / withdrawal | 1–3 months | Taxes + handling fees |

## Key risks

- Do not assume a will is "globally valid" — validity is assessed separately under each country's law;
- Do not ignore compulsory shares and marital property — parts of the will may be invalid;
- Do not expect a foreign will to work directly in Chinese procedures — local recognition steps are usually required;
- When in doubt, follow local law and professional advice.
