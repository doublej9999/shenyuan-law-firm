# 工厂延期交货，买家如何索赔？

货期从 45 天拖到 90 天，客户的订单在等，旺季的货架在空着——延期交货对外贸买家来说不只是"晚几天"，而是下游违约、订单流失、资金被占用的连锁反应。很多买家只会反复催，等工厂彻底交不出货时才发现：合同里既没有明确的交货日期，也没有违约金条款。

其实从法律上看，买家在延期交货后有一整套可用的权利，关键是按顺序、留证据、别错过时机。

## 第一步：核对合同，确认"交货期"到底怎么约定

- 合同是否写明交货日期或"预计交期"？注意区分"装运期"（shipment date）与"到货期"（delivery date）；
- 是否有"时间即要素"（time is of the essence）条款——有它，延期即构成根本违约，买家可以直接解除合同；
- INCOTERMS 2020 只解决风险与费用划分，不解决交货迟延的责任问题，责任认定仍看合同条款与适用法律。

## 第二步：书面催告，设定合理补救期

即使工厂已经延期，法律上通常仍要求买方先发出书面催告，给予一个合理的宽限期（常见 14–30 天），并明确告知：期满仍不交货将解除合同并索赔。催告要用邮件等可留存方式发出，写清楚原定交期、实际延误、最后期限与逾期后果。

## 第三步：行使解除权

宽限期满仍未交货，或工厂明确表示无法交货，买家可解除合同并要求返还已付款项。解除权的行使条件和后果依合同约定与适用法律（中国法下适用《民法典》合同编相关规定，外国法以当地法律为准）。

## 第四步：索赔损失

可主张的损失通常包括：已付货款及利息、替代采购的差价（cover purchase）、转售损失、因迟延产生的仓储与资金占用成本。注意：赔偿以"可预见性"为限——订立合同时工厂能合理预见的损失才可能获得支持，因此货物的特殊用途、转售安排最好在签约时书面写明。

## 第五步：违约金与定金条款

如果合同约定了逾期违约金（如每日万分之五）或定金罚则，按约定主张；没有约定时，按适用法律主张实际损失。中国法下违约金过高可被调低、过低可申请调高，具体以裁判机构裁量为准。

## 时间与费用预期

| 步骤 | 通常周期 | 主要成本 |
|------|---------|---------|
| 书面催告 | 1–2 周 | 律师函费（固定，较低） |
| 协商/和解 | 2–6 周 | 律师时费 |
| 仲裁/诉讼 | 6–18 个月 | 律师费+仲裁费/诉讼费 |
| 执行 | 6–24 个月 | 执行费用，视资产情况 |

以上为经验区间，实际取决于合同约定、法域与案件复杂度。我们不承诺结果，但可以帮您评估证据与索赔路径。

## 风险提示

- 不要无限期等待——多数法域对违约索赔有时效限制，拖久了可能丧失胜诉权；
- 不要只口头催货——所有沟通尽量书面化，尤其是对方承认延期的回复；
- 不要自行扣留工厂的其他货物抵债——可能构成新的违约甚至侵权；
- 收到工厂"再等一个月"的承诺时，要求其书面确认并保留追偿权利。

# Supplier Late Delivery: Buyer's Legal Remedies

The delivery date slipped from 45 days to 90. Your customers are waiting, and the shelves for peak season are staying empty. Late delivery is rarely "just a few days late" — it cascades into downstream defaults, lost orders, and capital tied up in goods that never arrive. Too many buyers respond by chasing the supplier with calls, only to discover at the end that the contract has no delivery date, no penalty clause, and no written trail.

Legally, a buyer facing late delivery has a full set of remedies. The key is to act in order, keep everything in writing, and not miss the deadlines.

## Step 1: Review the contract — what did the supplier actually promise?

- Check whether the contract states a shipment date, a delivery date, or only a vague "estimated lead time";
- Look for a "time is of the essence" clause — where it exists, delay is a fundamental breach and the buyer may terminate outright;
- Remember that INCOTERMS 2020 allocate risk and cost, not responsibility for delay. Liability for late delivery comes from the contract terms and the governing law.

## Step 2: Send a written notice and grant a cure period

Even after delay, most legal systems expect the buyer to give the supplier a written notice and a reasonable cure period (commonly 14–30 days). The notice should state the original delivery date, the delay, a final deadline, and that failure to deliver will trigger termination and damages. Send it by email or another traceable channel.

## Step 3: Terminate if the supplier fails to cure

If the cure period expires without delivery — or the supplier states it cannot deliver — the buyer may terminate the contract and demand repayment of amounts paid. The conditions and consequences of termination depend on the contract and the governing law (in China, the Civil Code's contract provisions; abroad, local law).

## Step 4: Claim damages

Recoverable losses typically include: paid amounts plus interest, the extra cost of a cover purchase, resale losses, and storage or financing costs caused by the delay. Damages are generally limited to losses the supplier could reasonably foresee at contracting — so if the goods were for a specific project or resale, say so in writing at the time of contracting.

## Step 5: Enforce penalty and deposit clauses

If the contract has a liquidated damages clause (for example, 0.05% per day of delay) or a deposit rule, claim under those terms. Without a clause, claim actual losses under the applicable law. Under Chinese law, courts may adjust penalties that are clearly excessive or too low.

## Key risks

- Do not wait indefinitely — most jurisdictions impose limitation periods on breach claims, and delay can forfeit the right to sue;
- Do not rely on verbal chasing — keep every exchange in writing, especially any admission of delay;
- Do not withhold the supplier's other goods as leverage — that can create a new breach or tort claim;
- If the supplier asks for "one more month," get a written confirmation while preserving your right to claim.
