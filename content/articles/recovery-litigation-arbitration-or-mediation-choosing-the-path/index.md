# 诉讼、仲裁还是调解：跨境纠纷怎么选？

对方拖欠货款、交付的伙伴中途变卦，你手里攥着合同，却在第一步就卡住——到底去法院起诉，申请仲裁，还是先坐下来调解？很多人凭感觉选，结果花了冤枉钱、拖了半年才发现"选错了场子"。跨境纠纷没有标准答案：诉讼公开但可上诉、周期长得吓人；仲裁一裁终局、易于跨境执行但费用高；调解快而便宜却缺乏强制力。选错路径，轻则周期翻倍，重则拿到的裁决无法执行，欠款彻底落空。下面把三条路的核心差别一次讲清，帮你按自己的案件做选择。

## 第一步：先看合同，再定方向

- **合同有争议解决条款，必须按约定走**：约定了仲裁（如 SIAC、HKIAC、ICC 或 CIETAC）就别去法院；约定了某国法院管辖就据此起诉；
- **没有约定时才有自由选择权**：此时才需要权衡诉讼、仲裁、调解各自利弊；
- **识别"真实债务人"与可执行财产在哪**：财产在哪个法域，就往哪走，这直接决定执行难度。

## 第二步：三条路径怎么选

- **协商/调解（Mediation/ADR）**：最短平快、保密、成本最低，适合争议仅在金额、双方仍想做生意的情况；缺点是没有强制力，对方不履约仍要回到仲裁或诉讼；
- **仲裁（Arbitration）**：一裁终局、当事人可自选仲裁地与仲裁员、程序保密，裁决依《纽约公约》在 170 多个国家执行，是跨境商事纠纷的"主流选择"；缺点是费用最高、取证程序相对受限；
- **诉讼（Litigation）**：公开、有完整上诉程序，判决在本国境内执行力强；但跨境执行判决往往要依赖双边司法协助安排，比仲裁裁决更难，且跨国诉讼周期长、成本难控。

## 第三步：按金额与目标对号入座

- **小额、想快速回款**：优先调解或低成本程序；争议金额大幅低于程序费用时，先谈判止损更划算；
- **中额、对方仍经营**：仲裁平衡速度与执行力，最稳妥；
- **大额、对方可能转移财产**：无论选哪条，第一时间申请财产保全/冻结令，避免"赢了裁决拿不到钱"。

## 时间与费用预期

| 路径 | 经验周期 | 主要成本 | 跨境执行力 |
|------|---------|---------|-----------|
| 协商/调解 | 数周至数月 | 低-中 | 靠协议履行 |
| 仲裁 | 6–24 个月 | 高（仲裁费+律师费） | 高（纽约公约） |
| 法院诉讼 | 12–36 个月 | 中-高 | 看双边安排 |

以上均为经验区间，具体取决于金额、复杂程度、对方所在地法与程序选择——**我们不承诺结果**，但"选对路径+及时保全"往往直接决定能否拿回欠款。

## 风险提示

- 切忌忽略合同里已经写好的争议解决条款，擅自选错路径会白费功夫；
- 不要把"能执行"想当然，先确认对方财产与所在法域的承认执行规则；
- 不要只看费用，调解虽便宜但可能换来一份执行不了的协议；
- 中国法律与外国法差异大，拿不准的以当地法律/专业意见为准。

# Litigation, Arbitration or Mediation: Choosing the Path

A buyer owes you money, or a counterparty has changed course mid-deal, and you are holding the contract but stuck at the very first decision — court, arbitration, or mediation? Choosing on instinct often means wasted fees and months lost in the wrong forum. There is no one-size-fits-all answer for cross-border disputes: litigation is public and appealable but slow; arbitration is final and travels well for enforcement but costs the most; mediation is quick and cheap but not binding. The wrong choice can double your timeline or — worse — produce a decision that cannot be enforced overseas, and the debt is gone. Here is a clear comparison so you can choose based on your own case.

## Step 1: read the contract and set direction

- **If there is a dispute-resolution clause, follow it**: an agreed arbitral forum (SIAC, HKIAC, ICC, CIETAC) means no court; an agreed court means litigation there;
- **Only when there is no clause do you have free choice**: that is when you weigh litigation, arbitration, and mediation;
- **Identify the true debtor and where enforceable assets sit**: the location of assets should drive your choice, as it dictates enforcement difficulty.

## Step 2: how to choose among the three paths

- **Settlement/mediation (ADR)**: fastest, confidential, lowest cost, and best when the dispute is only about amount and both sides want to keep trading; the catch is no binding force — if the other side reneges, you still go to arbitration or litigation;
- **Arbitration**: final and binding, with chosen venue and arbitrators and confidential proceedings; awards enforce in 170+ countries under the New York Convention, making it the mainstream choice for cross-border commercial disputes; downsides are the highest cost and limited discovery;
- **Litigation**: public, with a full appeal process and strong enforcement within the home country; but cross-border enforcement of judgments usually depends on bilateral judicial-assistance arrangements — harder than awards — and international litigation is long and hard to budget.

## Step 3: match the amount and your goal

- **Small amounts, fast recovery**: prefer mediation or low-cost procedures; when the amount is well below the procedural cost, negotiate and cut losses first;
- **Mid-size amounts, counterparty still operating**: arbitration balances speed and enforceability;
- **Large amounts, possible asset dissipation**: whichever path you take, apply promptly for preservation or freezing orders so a won award is not uncollectable.

## Time and cost expectations

| Path | Typical timeline | Main costs | Cross-border enforceability |
|------|------------------|------------|------------------------------|
| Settlement/mediation | Weeks to months | Low–medium | Depends on the agreement |
| Arbitration | 6–24 months | High (arbitration + counsel fees) | High (New York Convention) |
| Court litigation | 12–36 months | Medium–high | Depends on bilateral arrangements |

These are experience ranges; actual figures depend on the amount, complexity, counterparty location, and forum — **we do not promise results**, but choosing the right path and preserving assets early usually determines whether you recover.

## Key risks

- Do not ignore the dispute-resolution clause already in the contract and waste effort in the wrong forum;
- Do not assume "enforceable" — first confirm where the debtor's assets sit and the recognition rules in that jurisdiction;
- Do not judge by fees alone — mediation may be cheap but can yield an unenforceable agreement;
- China law and foreign law differ; when in doubt, follow local law and professional advice.
