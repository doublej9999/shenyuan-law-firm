# 海外亲属去世，国内继承人如何办理继承？

"父亲在海外去世，我在国内，遗产怎么办？"这是跨境继承咨询里最高频的问题。国内继承人办理海外继承，最大的障碍不是法律条文，而是**地理距离带来的程序鸿沟**：文件怎么公证、授权怎么做、钱怎么汇回来。本文按顺序梳理实操路径。

## 第1步：先判断"适用哪国法律、走哪国程序"

遗产继承原则上遵循**遗产所在地法 + 属人法协调**的框架：

- 不动产：通常按所在地法律处理（房产在哪国，走哪国程序）；
- 动产（存款、股票等）：可能与逝者国籍/经常居所有关，也可能按资产所在地，需个案判断；
- 中国国内遗产：按中国法律办理继承。

这一步的结论决定你后续联系哪国机构、准备哪些文件。

## 第2步：准备基础文件

国内继承人通常需要准备：

- 亲属关系证明（结婚证、出生证明、户籍档案、公证的亲属关系证明）；
- 逝者身份与死亡文件（死亡证明、护照/身份证明，需认证）；
- 遗嘱（如有）与资产线索（房产证、银行账户信息、股权凭证）；
- 全部继承人名单及身份文件——**继承人身份核实是跨境继承最常卡壳的环节**。

文件通常需要中英双语公证，并按目标国要求办理海牙认证或领事认证。

## 第3步：海外程序：认证与遗产管理

- 有遗嘱的，先在遗嘱所在国办理遗嘱认证（Probate），由遗嘱执行人处理遗产；
- 无遗嘱的，由当地法院指定遗产管理人；
- 国内继承人作为受益人，通常需要：提交公证文件、签署弃权或同意文件、配合资产清单核实；
- 部分国家要求继承人亲自到场或面谈，可尝试通过授权委托书（Power of Attorney）或领事渠道处理，但**授权文件本身也需要公证认证**。

## 第4步：国内资产的同步处理

逝者在国内的房产、存款、股权，需按中国继承法办理：全部继承人协商一致后办理继承公证（或诉讼确认），再凭继承权证书办理过户/提取。**海外程序与国内程序可以并行，但文件（尤其亲属关系与死亡证明）往往是同一套，先备齐一次公证认证最省成本。**

## 第5步：资金合规汇回

- 继承款汇回中国属于经常项目/资本项目下的特定情形，需提供继承权证明、完税证明等材料；
- 大额资金建议提前与银行确认材料要求；
- 涉及海外遗产税义务的，需在分配前完成申报缴纳，否则可能影响资金出境。

## 常见卡点清单

| 卡点 | 后果 | 对策 |
|------|------|------|
| 亲属关系证明不齐全 | 程序停滞 | 提前调取户籍档案、出生公证 |
| 文件认证方式错误 | 被退回重办 | 先确认目标国要求（海牙认证 or 领事认证） |
| 继承人失联/不配合 | 无法出具全体同意 | 尽早联系全部继承人，必要时诉讼解决 |
| 授权委托不规范 | 无法代办 | 委托书做公证认证，明确授权范围 |
| 时效延误 | 资产被收归/程序复杂化 | 尽早启动，关注各国时限 |

## 给国内继承人的建议

1. **先做"法律适用"判断，再动手**——避免在错误的法域提交错误的文件；
2. **文件一次备齐**：亲属关系、死亡证明、遗嘱、资产凭证统一公证认证；
3. **国内外并行推进**：海外认证与国内继承公证共用一套文件，同步启动；
4. **资金安排提前规划**：税务与外汇合规早确认，避免"继承办完钱汇不出"。

# Relative Dies Abroad: How Heirs in China Claim Inheritance

"My father passed away abroad and I live in China — what happens to the estate?" This is the most common question in cross-border inheritance consultations. For heirs in China, the real obstacle is rarely the law itself; it is the **procedural gap created by distance**: how to notarize documents, how to authorize someone, how to get the money home. This article maps the practical path in order.

## Step 1: Determine which law and which procedure apply

Inheritance generally follows a framework coordinating **the law of the asset's location with personal law**:

- Real estate: governed by the law where it sits (property in country X means that country's procedure);
- Movables (deposits, shares): may relate to the deceased's nationality or habitual residence, or the asset's location — assessed case by case;
- China-situated assets: under Chinese inheritance law.

This conclusion determines which country's authorities you deal with and which documents you need.

## Step 2: Prepare the basic documents

Heirs in China typically need:

- Relationship proof (marriage certificates, birth certificates, household records, notarized kinship certificates);
- The deceased's identity and death documents (death certificate, passport/ID — legalized);
- The will (if any) and asset leads (property certificates, bank details, equity vouchers);
- The full list of heirs and their identity documents — **heir verification is the most common sticking point in cross-border inheritance.**

Documents are usually bilingual-notarized and then legalized (apostille or consular) as required by the destination country.

## Step 3: Overseas procedure: probate and estate administration

- With a will: probate in the will's jurisdiction first; the executor administers the estate;
- Without a will: the local court appoints an administrator;
- Heirs in China typically must: submit notarized documents, sign waivers or consents, and cooperate with asset verification;
- Some countries require heirs to appear in person — a Power of Attorney or consular channels may help, but **the authorization itself also needs notarization and legalization.**

## Step 4: Handle China assets in parallel

The deceased's China property, deposits, and equity are inherited under Chinese law: all heirs must agree (inheritance notarization or court confirmation), then transfer or withdraw using the inheritance certificate. **Overseas and domestic procedures can run in parallel — they often share the same document set, so one round of notarization and legalization saves real cost.**

## Step 5: Remit funds compliantly

- Inherited funds remitted to China fall under specific current/capital account circumstances; provide inheritance proof and tax-clearance documents;
- Confirm material requirements with your bank in advance for large amounts;
- Where foreign estate taxes apply, file and pay before distribution — otherwise funds may not leave the country.

## Common sticking points

| Sticking point | Consequence | Counter-measure |
|----------------|-------------|-----------------|
| Incomplete kinship proof | Proceedings stall | Retrieve household records, birth certificates early |
| Wrong legalization method | Documents rejected | Confirm requirements first (apostille vs consular) |
| Missing or uncooperative heirs | No unanimous consent | Contact all heirs early; litigate if necessary |
| Non-compliant authorizations | Cannot act on your behalf | Notarize the POA with clear scope |
| Delay | Assets escheat / complexity grows | Start early; mind each country's time limits |

## Advice for heirs in China

1. **Resolve the applicable-law question first** — avoid filing wrong documents in the wrong jurisdiction;
2. **Prepare documents in one pass**: kinship, death certificate, will, asset vouchers — notarized and legalized together;
3. **Run overseas and domestic tracks in parallel**, sharing one document set;
4. **Plan the funds early**: confirm tax and FX compliance before distribution, so the money can actually move.
