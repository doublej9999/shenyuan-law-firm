# 管辖条款与法律适用条款怎么谈才不吃亏？

很多出口企业签合同时，最关心价格、交期、付款方式，却把管辖条款和法律适用条款当成"格式条款"直接跳过。直到纠纷发生才发现：要在对方国家的法院、按对方国家的法律打官司，光是翻译、公证、聘请当地律师的成本就够买一批货了。这两条条款，就是决定"战场在哪、用什么规则打"的条款，谈判时多花十分钟，可能省下几十万。

## 第一步：先分清两个"管"的区别

- **管辖条款（Jurisdiction）**：决定纠纷由哪个国家/地区的法院或仲裁机构审理。例如约定"上海国际经济贸易仲裁委员会（SHIAC）仲裁"或"新加坡国际仲裁中心（SIAC）仲裁"。
- **法律适用条款（Governing Law）**：决定审理时适用哪一国的实体法。例如约定"本合同适用中华人民共和国法律"或"适用英国法"。

两者可以分开约定，也可以组合约定。常见的错误是只写了管辖地、没写适用法律，结果在约定法院打官司，法官却可能按冲突法规则适用了对方的国内法。

## 第二步：谈判时的三个核心主张

1. **争取本国/中立地仲裁**：对中方出口企业而言，首选是中国内地仲裁机构（如贸仲CIETAC、SHIAC），次选香港、新加坡等中立地仲裁。对方通常也会要求"主场"，这是正常的谈判拉锯。
2. **法律适用优先选熟悉的法律**：中国法、香港法、新加坡法都是建立在成文法或成熟判例体系上的，相对可预期。尽量避免接受完全陌生的法域，除非有当地律师把关。
3. **与付款条件联动**：可以把管辖条款和付款方式绑定谈判——例如接受对方的付款条件，就要求管辖地放到对自己有利的地方；反之亦然。

## 第三步：识别常见陷阱

- **"双向"条款被写成"单向"**：例如只写"卖方可在买方所在地起诉"，买方却只能去卖方所在地，形成不对等；
- **管辖地与执行地脱节**：选了 A 国仲裁，但对方资产在 B 国，裁决还要到 B 国申请执行，多一层程序；
- **"或"字条款模糊不清**：写了"可在 A 地或 B 地"，一旦对方先起诉，你被动应诉，成本翻倍；
- **无效管辖约定**：部分法域对消费者合同、格式合同中的管辖条款有特殊限制，约定不当可能被认定无效。

## 第四步：写清楚，写完整

条款至少应包含：争议解决方式（诉讼/仲裁二选一，不要混写）、机构名称与规则版本、仲裁地（seat）、仲裁语言、适用法律、是否排除《联合国国际货物销售合同公约》（CISG）的适用。这些细节直接影响程序成本和结果可预期性，建议请律师审阅后定稿。

## 风险提示

- 不要在未读条款的情况下用对方模板直接签约，尤其是小字部分的争议解决条款；
- 不要以为"反正打官司概率低"——条款是给最坏情况准备的；
- 若对方坚持其主场，务必让当地律师评估该法域的执行成本与胜诉后回款难度，再做让步决定。

# Jurisdiction & Governing Law Clauses: How to Negotiate

Many exporters negotiate price, delivery dates, and payment terms carefully — then sign off on the jurisdiction and governing law clauses without reading them. When a dispute breaks out, they discover they must litigate in the buyer's country under the buyer's law, and the translation, notarization, and local counsel fees alone can exceed the value of a shipment. These two clauses decide where the battle is fought and under what rules. Ten minutes of negotiation now can save hundreds of thousands later.

## Step 1: Understand the two clauses

- **Jurisdiction clause**: decides which court or arbitral tribunal hears the dispute — for example, arbitration at the Shanghai International Economic and Trade Arbitration Commission (SHIAC) or the Singapore International Arbitration Centre (SIAC).
- **Governing law clause**: decides which country's substantive law applies — for example, "this contract is governed by PRC law" or "governed by English law."

The two can be agreed separately or combined. A common mistake is specifying the forum but not the law, leaving the judge to apply conflict-of-law rules that may end up applying the other party's domestic law.

## Step 2: Three core positions in negotiation

1. **Push for home or neutral arbitration**: for Chinese exporters, first choice is mainland arbitration (CIETAC, SHIAC), second is a neutral seat such as Hong Kong or Singapore. Expect the counterparty to demand its own home turf — this is normal bargaining.
2. **Choose a law you understand**: PRC, Hong Kong, and Singapore law are relatively predictable. Avoid accepting a completely unfamiliar jurisdiction without local counsel review.
3. **Link the clause to payment terms**: trade concessions on payment for a favorable forum, or vice versa.

## Step 3: Watch for common traps

- **One-sided drafting**: the clause lets the seller sue in the buyer's country but forces the buyer to sue in the seller's country;
- **Forum disconnected from assets**: arbitrating in country A while the debtor's assets sit in country B adds an entire enforcement layer;
- **Ambiguous "or" clauses**: "may sue in A or B" lets the counterparty choose the forum, doubling your costs;
- **Invalid clauses**: some jurisdictions restrict jurisdiction clauses in consumer or standard-form contracts — an invalid clause may leave you with no agreed forum at all.

## Step 4: Draft it completely

The clause should specify: the dispute method (arbitration or litigation — pick one, never both), the institution and its rules version, the seat, the language, the governing law, and whether the CISG is excluded. These details drive procedural cost and predictability. Have counsel review before signing.

## Risks to avoid

- Never sign a counterparty template without reading the dispute resolution clause, especially the fine print;
- Do not assume disputes are unlikely — these clauses exist for the worst case;
- If the counterparty insists on its home forum, have local counsel assess enforcement costs and realistic recovery before conceding.
