# 货物质量不合格：拒收还是索赔？

货柜到港，开箱验货时你发现一批产品尺寸、材质都与合同约定不符，部分还有明显缺陷。业务员催你尽快提柜，报关行催你清关，可这批货到底"收还是拒"，你拿不定主意。这个决定直接影响你后续的索赔资格：错过了检验通知期限，法律上可能被视为接受货物；拒收动作不规范，又可能反被供应商以违约追责。拖延几天，往往就把一桩明确的索赔案拖成了僵局。

## 先弄清：书面合同怎么约定的

- 查看合同是否约定质量异议期、检验期限与检验地，正常做法是收货后尽快检验并书面提出异议；
- 若约定适用联合国《货物销售公约》，买方应在合理期限内检验并通知不符，否则可能丧失主张权；
- 合同约定不明的，以适用法及交易习惯为准，拿不准请当地律师把关。

## 第1步：及时检验并固定证据

- 到货后立即安排抽样检验，保留封存样品、照片、视频与第三方检验报告；
- 对数量短少、外观损坏与内在质量问题分别记录，区分"毁损"与"质量不符"是两个不同索赔方向；
- 全部证据注明时间、地点、批次，越具体越有利。

## 第2步：在期限内书面提出异议

- 在合同或法律规定的期限内，以书面形式向供应商正式提出质量异议并主张权利；
- 明确表达"保留追究权利"而非简单催办，避免被视为接受货物；
- 必要时同时通知货运代理、保险公司与银行（尤其涉及信用证时），保护相关权利。

## 第3步：权衡拒收还是索赔

- 拒收：适用于货物无法使用或修复成本过高，需及时通知供应商并妥善保管货物，配合其复检；
- 索赔：货物部分可用或修复后可用时，可主张降价、违约金、返修或返运费用及因此产生的损失；
- 注意"可分割拒收"：对部分货物可按合同只拒收不符批次，避免全单作废。

## 第4步：评估补救与后续处置

- 协商返修、补货、降价或退款，签署和解书面确认；
- 协商不成，依据合同争议解决条款提起仲裁或诉讼，主张损失赔偿（以合同约定与实际损失为限）。

## 时间与费用预期

| 环节 | 经验周期 | 主要成本 |
|------|---------|---------|
| 到货检验与证据固定 | 1–2 周 | 检验费/第三方报告费 |
| 书面异议与协商 | 2 周–2 个月 | 律师函/沟通成本 |
| 返修、退款或和解 | 1–6 个月 | 按实际安排 |
| 仲裁/诉讼索赔 | 6–18 个月 | 律师费+仲裁/诉讼费 |

以上均为经验区间，取决于证据与争议解决方式。我们不承诺结果，但异议提得及时、证据留得完整，是争取有利结果的前提。

## 风险提示

- 不要在检验通知期限届满后才提出异议，这是索赔失败最常见原因；
- 不要未经通知就擅自处置或变卖货物，可能丧失索赔依据并反被追责；
- 不要混淆"运输毁损"与"质量不符"，两者索赔对象与路径不同；
- 各国质量标准、拒收规则与检验义务差异大，具体以合同与当地法律为准。

# Defective Goods: Rejection vs Damages

The container has arrived and, during inspection, you find a batch of products whose dimensions and materials do not match the contract — some with obvious defects. Your staff urge you to move the container out and clear customs, but you are unsure whether to "accept or reject." That decision will shape your right to claim: miss the time limit for notifying defects and the goods may be deemed accepted as a matter of law; reject improperly and you may face a cross-claim for breach instead. A few days of hesitation can turn a clear case into a stalemate.

## First, read the contract

- Check for a quality objection period, inspection deadline, and place of inspection; the usual rule is to inspect promptly on arrival and object in writing.
- Where the CISG applies, the buyer must inspect within a reasonable time and notify of non-conformity or risk losing the claim.
- If silent, the applicable law and trade usage govern — when unsure, confirm with local counsel.

## Step 1: Inspect and fix the evidence promptly

- Arrange sampling promptly after arrival; keep sealed samples, photos, video, and third-party inspection reports.
- Record shortage, apparent damage, and latent quality issues separately — "damage in transit" and "non-conformity" are distinct claims.
- Date, place, and batch every item of evidence; the more specific, the stronger your position.

## Step 2: Object in writing within the deadline

- Formally notify the supplier of the quality issue and assert your rights within the contract or statutory deadline.
- State expressly that you "reserve your rights" rather than merely chasing a reply, to avoid being treated as having accepted the goods.
- Where relevant, also notify the freight forwarder, insurer, and bank (especially under a letter of credit) to protect related rights.

## Step 3: Weigh rejection versus damages

- Reject: where the goods are unusable or repair costs are excessive; notify promptly, keep the goods safely, and allow the supplier's re-inspection.
- Claim damages: where some goods are usable or repairable, claim a price reduction, penalty, repair or return costs, and resulting losses.
- Consider severable rejection: reject only the non-conforming lot where the contract permits, avoiding cancellation of the whole order.

## Step 4: Resolve and follow up

- Negotiate repair, replacement, price reduction, or refund; document any settlement in writing.
- If negotiation fails, pursue arbitration or litigation under the dispute resolution clause, claiming damages limited to the contract and actual loss.

## Time and cost expectations

| Stage | Typical range | Main costs |
|-------|---------------|------------|
| Inspection and evidence | 1–2 weeks | Inspection and third-party report fees |
| Notice and negotiation | 2 weeks–2 months | Attorney letter / communication |
| Repair, refund, or settlement | 1–6 months | As arranged |
| Arbitration / litigation | 6–18 months | Counsel and arbitration/court fees |

These are experience ranges and depend on the evidence and chosen forum. We do not promise outcomes, but timely objections and complete evidence are the precondition for a favorable result.

## Key risks

- Do not raise the objection after the notice deadline — the most common reason claims fail.
- Do not dispose of or sell the goods before notice; it can destroy the claim and invite counterclaims.
- Do not confuse "damage in transit" with "quality non-conformity"; they target different parties and paths.
- Quality standards, rejection rules, and inspection duties differ by country; follow the contract and local law.
