# 追债公司 vs 律师：为什么建议选择律师

对方拖欠的那笔货款拖了半年，你说服不了、又不想花高价打官司，于是有人建议："找家追债公司，便宜又快。"听起来省心，可真到了跨境追收，追债公司与律师手里的"牌"完全不同。你现在的每一次拖延，都在给对方转移资产、注销公司、躲过执行留时间。选错工具不但要不到钱，还可能把仅剩的诉讼时效和保全窗口一起耗掉。下面把两者的区别、各自的适用场景和正确路径讲清楚。

## 第一步：先分清追债公司与律师的根本区别

- **追债公司**（collection agency）本质是"催收服务商"，多数只能通过电话、函件、上门施压等方式催促债务人还款，一般**没有起诉、申请财产保全、申请强制执行的法律资格**；
- **律师**具有执业资格，能以专业身份发出具有法律效力的律师函，并代理诉讼、仲裁、保全与执行；
- 关键提醒：不同国家/地区对"催收行为"的监管差异很大，资质、收费和话术红线都不同，涉外追收务必先确认对方的合法资质。

## 第二步：判断这笔债是否已经"超出追债公司的能力"

- 若债务人还在经营、愿意沟通、欠款金额较小，谈判式催收也许能解决；
- 但只要出现以下任一情形，追债公司往往就"够不着"了：对方失联、转移资产、注销公司、扯皮时效、货证不符、需要冻结账户——这些都需要**诉讼与执行手段**；
- 出现上述情形还继续依赖催收，等于把最宝贵的时间窗口让出去，钱可能再也追不回。

## 第三步：把案件交给律师，律师能做什么

- **合法施压与取证**：发出正式律师函，并协助固定合同、提单、对账、聊天记录等证据；涉外案件还能指引公证认证与翻译；
- **保全与止付**：在有明确依据时申请冻结令/保全，阻止对方转移资产——这往往是决定能否追回的关键一步；
- **诉讼或仲裁**：判断适用哪国法律与管辖，走法院或仲裁程序，并处理判决/裁决的跨境承认与执行；
- **合规与文书**：跨境追收涉及多法域，涉及政策、格式与时效，交由专业人士按当地法律办理，以当地专业意见为准。

## 第四步：如何选择与费用对比

- 选聘时核对对方是否在中国及债务人所在地均具备执业资质，费用通常按"小时费、阶段费或追回后分成"约定，务必以书面协议明确；
- 追债公司收费看似低，但多数按"追回金额"分成，且**没有诉讼/执行能力**；真正要打官司时你仍得再请律师，反而多付一层成本；
- 把"能不能起诉、能不能查封"作为核心判断标准，而不是只看价格。

## 时间与费用预期

| 路径 | 经验周期 | 主要成本 |
|------|---------|---------|
| 律师函与证据梳理 | 数天至2周 | 低（固定咨询/函件费） |
| 谈判/协商 | 数天至1个月 | 低到中 |
| 财产保全/冻结 | 依法域 | 保全担保+律师费 |
| 诉讼/仲裁至执行 | 3–12个月不等 | 律师费+诉讼/执行费 |

以上均为经验区间，实际取决于案件复杂度、对方态度与所在法域——我们不承诺结果，但"是否及时采取法律行动"往往决定成败。

## 风险提示

- 不要只图"便宜"选没有诉讼能力的机构，错过时效与保全窗口；
- 不要拖欠太久才行动，越早固定证据、采取行动越有利；
- 不要被"保证追回""承诺回款"等话术误导——请对方书面说明资质、手段与收费，谨防二次损失；
- 涉外追收务必区分中国法律与当地法律，拿不准的以当地法律/专业意见为准。

# Collection Agencies vs Lawyers: Why Legal Counsel Wins

The money that buyer owes you has been outstanding for six months. You cannot talk them into paying, and you would rather not spend on a lawsuit, so someone suggests: "Try a collection agency — cheap and quick." It sounds easy, but in cross-border recovery, collection agencies and lawyers are simply not playing with the same hand. Every month of delay gives the debtor time to move assets, wind up the company, or outlast the limitation period. Choosing the wrong tool not only fails to recover the money — it can burn the remaining window for preservation and suit. Here is the difference, when each makes sense, and the right path forward.

## Step 1: understand the fundamental difference

- A **collection agency** is essentially a debt-chasing service: in most cases it can only press the debtor by phone, letters, or on-site contact, and it generally **lacks the legal authority to sue, apply for preservation, or enforce a judgment**;
- A **lawyer** is licensed to act as counsel: issuing a formal demand letter with legal weight, and handling litigation, arbitration, preservation, and enforcement;
- Key note: regulation of "debt collection" varies widely by country — licensing, fees, and forbidden tactics differ, so verify the agency's legal standing before relying on it cross-border.

## Step 2: know when a debtor is beyond an agency's reach

- If the debtor is still operating, willing to talk, and the amount is small, negotiation-style chasing may work;
- But as soon as any of these appear, an agency usually hits its limit: the debtor goes silent, moves assets, winds up the company, argues limitation, disputes the goods, or needs assets frozen — all of which demand **litigation and enforcement tools**;
- Continuing to rely on chasing once these arise is simply giving away the most valuable time, and the money may never come back.

## Step 3: what a lawyer can actually do

- **Lawful pressure and evidence**: issue a formal demand letter and help fix in place contracts, bills of lading, reconciliations, and chat records; for overseas matters, counsel can also direct notarization, apostille, and translation;
- **Freezing and preservation**: where there is a proper basis, seek injunctions or preservation to stop the debtor moving assets — often the decisive step in whether you recover at all;
- **Litigation or arbitration**: determine the governing law and forum, run the court or arbitral process, and handle cross-border recognition and enforcement of the judgment or award;
- **Compliance and paperwork**: cross-border recovery touches several legal systems; documents, formats, and time limits differ, so have it handled by professionals under local law, on local professional advice.

## Step 4: choosing and comparing costs

- When selecting counsel, confirm licensing in both China and the debtor's location; fees are usually agreed on an hourly, stage, or contingency basis — always put them in a written engagement;
- An agency's headline fee may look low, but most charge a share of what is recovered and **cannot sue or enforce**; when you ultimately need to litigate you would still have to hire a lawyer, paying an extra layer;
- Judge by whether the firm can sue and freeze assets, not by price alone.

## Time and cost expectations

| Route | Typical timeline | Main costs |
|-------|------------------|------------|
| Demand letter and evidence review | Days to 2 weeks | Low (consult/fee) |
| Negotiation / settlement | Days to 1 month | Low to medium |
| Preservation / freezing | Jurisdiction-dependent | Security + counsel fees |
| Litigation/arbitration to enforcement | 3–12 months, varies | Counsel + litigation/enforcement fees |

These are experience ranges; actual figures depend on complexity, the debtor's attitude, and the jurisdiction — we do not promise results, but whether legal action is taken in time often decides the outcome.

## Key risks

- Do not choose a "cheap" firm with no litigation capacity and miss the limitation and preservation windows;
- Do not delay until it is too late — acting early to fix evidence pays off;
- Do not be swayed by "guaranteed recovery" or "promised repayment" talk — ask for the firm's qualifications, methods, and fees in writing to avoid a second loss;
- For overseas matters, distinguish China law from local law, and when in doubt follow local law/professional advice.
