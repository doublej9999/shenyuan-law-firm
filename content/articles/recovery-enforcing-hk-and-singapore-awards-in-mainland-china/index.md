# 新加坡/香港仲裁裁决在内地的执行

你在香港国际仲裁中心（HKIAC）或新加坡国际仲裁中心（SIAC）打赢了仲裁，裁决书清清楚楚写着对方该付多少钱。可高兴没多久你就发现：债务人的主要资产在深圳、上海，香港和新加坡的裁决在内地法院不认账就没意义。于是案例卡在了"裁决有了、钱拿不到"这一关。其实内地承认与执行香港、新加坡仲裁裁决有明确的法律通道，关键在于选对依据、材料齐全、程序不走错，否则就可能被以"程序瑕疵"等理由驳回。下面按步骤拆解，让你少走弯路。

## 第一步：先确认适用哪条法律依据

- **香港裁决**：依据《最高人民法院关于内地与香港特别行政区相互执行仲裁裁决的安排》，向债务人住所地或财产所在地的内地中级人民法院申请。\\
- **新加坡裁决**：中国是 1958 年《纽约公约》缔约国，可依公约向内地有管辖权的中级人民法院申请承认与执行。\\
- 两条路径都要求裁决在来源地已具终局效力且未被撤销，所以要一并准备裁决受理方出具的终局性证明。

## 第二步：确定有管辖权的内地法院并准备材料

- **管辖法院**：一般是被申请人住所地或可供执行财产所在地的中级人民法院；财产分散时可择一处启动。\\
- **核心材料**：承认与执行申请书、仲裁协议、裁决书正本或经认证的副本、以及对应的中文翻译件，外文文件通常需公证认证或由有资格机构出具译本。\\
- **特别提示**：材料缺一份都可能被要求补正，导致周期拉长，务必一次性备齐。

## 第三步：启动申请并跟进审查程序

- 向法院提交申请后，法院会送达被申请人并给予书面答辩期；\\
- 法院重点审查是否属于《纽约公约》第五条或两地安排明确的拒绝事由（如仲裁协议无效、程序未获适当通知、裁决超裁等）；\\
- 审查通过后作出承认/执行裁定，再进入具体的执行程序（查封、冻结、拍卖等）。

## 时间与费用预期

| 环节 | 经验周期 | 主要成本 |
|------|---------|---------|
| 材料准备与翻译、公证 | 数周 | 翻译/公证认证费 |
| 法院审查与裁定 | 通常 3-12 个月，视法院与案情 | 申请费（按标的比例）+律师费 |
| 裁定后的强制执行 | 数月，视财产线索 | 执行费用+财产处置成本 |

以上均为经验区间，取决于标的金额、法域与财产状况。**我们不承诺结果**，但材料越齐全、财产线索越明确，整体推进越快。

## 风险提示

- 被申请人常以"仲裁程序未获适当通知""裁决超裁"等理由抗辩，务必保证程序证据完整；\\
- 承认与执行是两个环节，先拿到承认裁定再谈执行，不要混为一步；\\
- 外国法事项（如新加坡仲裁法细节）以当地专业意见为准；\\
- 对方可能提前转移资产，若担心，可在申请前评估财产保全的可行性。

# Enforcing HK and Singapore Awards in Mainland China

You won an arbitration at HKIAC or the Singapore International Arbitration Centre (SIAC), and the award clearly states how much the other side must pay. But the relief quickly fades when you realise the debtor's main assets sit in Shenzhen or Shanghai—an award no mainland court will honour is meaningless. Cases stall at "award in hand, no money received." The good news: mainland China has clear legal channels to recognise and enforce both Hong Kong and Singapore awards. The key is choosing the right legal basis, preparing complete documents, and following procedure correctly, or you risk dismissal on grounds such as procedural defects. Here is a step-by-step breakdown.

## Step 1: identify the applicable legal basis

- **Hong Kong awards**: under the Arrangement between the Mainland and the Hong Kong SAR on mutual enforcement of arbitral awards, apply to the intermediate people's court at the debtor's domicile or property location. \\
- **Singapore awards**: China is a party to the 1958 New York Convention, so awards can be recognised and enforced through the convention route at the competent intermediate people's court. \\
- Both routes require that the award is final and not set aside in its seat, so also obtain a certificate of finality from the issuing authority.

## Step 2: determine the competent court and prepare documents

- **Court**: generally the intermediate people's court at the respondent's domicile or the location of executable property; where assets are scattered, choose one location to start. \\
- **Key documents**: the application for recognition and enforcement, the arbitration agreement, the original or certified copy of the award, and Chinese translations—foreign documents usually need notarisation/apostille or a qualified translation. \\
- **Tip**: a missing document can force supplementation and extend the timeline, so prepare everything up front.

## Step 3: file the application and follow the review process

- After filing, the court serves the respondent and allows a written defence period. \\
- The court focuses on grounds for refusal under Article V of the New York Convention or the Arrangement (invalid agreement, inadequate notice, ultra vires award, and so on). \\
- On approval, the court issues a recognition/enforcement ruling, then enforcement procedures begin (seizure, freezing, auction).

## Time and cost expectations

| Stage | Typical timeline | Main costs |
|-------|------------------|------------|
| Document preparation and translation | Weeks | Translation/notarisation fees |
| Court review and ruling | Usually 3-12 months, by court and case | Filing fees (based on amount) + legal fees |
| Enforcement after ruling | Months, by asset leads | Execution costs + asset disposal costs |

These are experience ranges, depending on amount, jurisdiction, and asset position. **We do not promise results**, but complete documents and clear asset leads speed the process.

## Key risks

- Respondents often argue "no proper notice" or "ultra vires award"—keep procedural evidence complete; \\
- Recognition and enforcement are separate stages: secure the recognition ruling first; \\
- Foreign-law matters such as Singapore arbitration rules should follow local professional advice; \\
- The other side may move assets early; if concerned, assess the feasibility of preservation before filing.
