# 国际买卖合同审查清单：10个必改条款

国际买卖合同的纠纷，90%都能追溯到签合同时没改的几个条款。很多出口商习惯用客户提供的合同模板——对方起草的合同，风险自然偏向对方。这份清单列出10个必查条款，签约前逐条过一遍，能省下后面大量的追收成本。

## 1. 价格与付款条款

- 明确币种、汇率风险承担方、付款节点（定金、发货前、见提单副本、到港、验收后）；
- 避免笼统的"payment terms to be agreed"——留白等于把主动权交给对方；
- T/T、L/C、D/P等结算方式的选择直接影响风险，建议结合客户信用状况配置。

## 2. 交付条款与INCOTERMS

- 写明贸易术语版本（如 INCOTERMS 2020）和具体交货港/目的港；
- FOB、CIF、DDP的风险与费用分界完全不同，选错可能多承担运费、关税和风险；
- 明确交货期限及"按时交货"的判定标准（以提单日/签收日为准）。

## 3. 质量标准与检验

- 质量标准要可测量：明确适用标准（国标、行标、客户规格书）与关键指标；
- 约定检验方式：第三方检验（SGS等）、验货时间、地点、异议期限；
- 保留"样品确认"记录，避免"以样品为准"这种事后扯皮条款。

## 4. 违约责任与违约金

- 明确迟延交货、迟延付款、质量不合格各自的违约责任；
- 违约金比例要合理（部分法域对过高违约金不予支持）；
- 尽量约定"实际损失+违约金"的双轨索赔路径。

## 5. 责任限制条款

- 对方合同常包含"赔偿责任以合同金额为限""排除间接损失"等条款；
- 若接受，务必评估：对方违约造成你的停产、转售损失是否会被排除；
- 核心义务（付款、交付）建议排除在责任限制之外。

## 6. 知识产权与保密

- 明确图纸、模具、专有技术归谁所有，使用范围与期限；
- OEM/ODM订单尤其要注意：客户的设计、商标、专利侵权责任由谁承担；
- 保密条款覆盖期限、范围和违约后果。

## 7. 不可抗力与合同受阻

- 不可抗力范围要具体（自然灾害、战争、政府行为、疫情等）；
- 明确通知时限、减损义务和解除条件；
- 跨境交易中，客户所在地的"不可抗力"主张差异很大，需预判。

## 8. 管辖与法律适用

- **这是最关键的条款**：约定仲裁（推荐常见仲裁机构）通常比诉讼更利于跨境执行；
- 明确适用法律（如中国法/英国法/新加坡法），避免冲突法上的不确定性；
- 不要接受"在对方国家法院诉讼+对方国家法律"的组合——除非你有当地执行资源。

## 9. 争议解决前的程序

- 约定先行协商/调解期（如30天），再进入仲裁或诉讼；
- 约定证据规则：书面沟通（邮件/微信/WhatsApp）作为有效证据；
- 小额争议可约定快速程序或独任仲裁员，控制成本。

## 10. 完整协议与变更条款

- 明确"本合同构成完整协议，先前的邮件、口头承诺不具效力"；
- 变更须书面并经双方签署——防止后续"口头追加订单"纠纷；
- 附件的效力与签署方式（报价单、规格书、通用条款）也要写明。

## 审查后如何落地

- 把清单变成公司自己的"标准合同模板"，每次谈判只改参数；
- 对方坚持用其模板时，逐条比对，把风险条款标注出来谈判；
- 拿不准的条款，在签约前做一次合同审查——成本远低于事后诉讼。

# International Sales Contract Review: 10 Clauses You Must Check

Ninety percent of international sales contract disputes trace back to a handful of clauses that were never fixed at signing. Many exporters work from the buyer's template — and a contract drafted by the other side naturally favors the other side. Run this checklist before signing and you will save a great deal of recovery cost later.

## 1. Price and payment terms

- Specify currency, FX risk allocation, and payment milestones (deposit, pre-shipment, against B/L copy, on arrival, post-inspection);
- Avoid vague language like "payment terms to be agreed" — blank space hands control to the counterparty;
- Choose T/T, L/C, or D/P deliberately, aligned with the buyer's credit profile.

## 2. Delivery terms and INCOTERMS

- State the INCOTERMS version (e.g. INCOTERMS 2020) and the exact port of loading/destination;
- FOB, CIF, and DDP split risk and cost very differently — the wrong term can add freight, duties, and risk;
- Define the delivery deadline and how "on time" is measured (B/L date, delivery receipt).

## 3. Quality standards and inspection

- Make quality measurable: applicable standards (national, industry, customer spec) and key metrics;
- Agree inspection mechanics: third-party inspection (e.g. SGS), timing, place, and objection deadlines;
- Keep signed sample approvals — avoid vague "per sample" clauses that breed disputes.

## 4. Breach and liquidated damages

- Define the consequences of late delivery, late payment, and defective goods separately;
- Keep penalty amounts reasonable — some jurisdictions refuse excessive penalties;
- Try for a dual path: proven damages plus liquidated damages.

## 5. Limitation of liability

- Buyer templates often cap liability at the contract price and exclude indirect losses;
- Before accepting, assess whether your lost production or resale losses would be excluded;
- Exclude core obligations (payment, delivery) from any limitation cap.

## 6. IP and confidentiality

- Specify who owns drawings, tooling, and know-how, plus scope and duration of use;
- For OEM/ODM orders, allocate infringement liability for the customer's designs, trademarks, and patents;
- Set confidentiality scope, term, and consequences.

## 7. Force majeure and frustration

- Define the force majeure list concretely (natural disasters, war, government action, pandemic);
- State notice deadlines, mitigation duties, and termination conditions;
- Force majeure claims vary widely by the buyer's jurisdiction — plan ahead.

## 8. Jurisdiction and governing law — the most critical clause

- Arbitration (with a recognized institution) usually enforces better across borders than litigation;
- Specify the governing law (China law, English law, Singapore law, etc.) to avoid conflict-of-law uncertainty;
- Do not accept "sue in the buyer's courts under the buyer's law" unless you have local enforcement resources.

## 9. Pre-dispute procedure

- Agree a cooling-off negotiation/mediation period (e.g. 30 days) before proceedings;
- Confirm that written communications (email, WeChat, WhatsApp) count as evidence;
- For small claims, opt for fast-track procedures or a sole arbitrator to control cost.

## 10. Entire agreement and amendments

- State that the contract is the complete agreement, superseding prior emails and verbal promises;
- Require written, signed amendments — prevents "verbal add-on order" disputes;
- Define the status of exhibits (quotations, specifications, general terms).

## Making the checklist stick

- Turn the checklist into your own standard template; renegotiate only parameters;
- If the buyer insists on their template, compare clause by clause and flag the risk items;
- When unsure, get a contract review before signing — cheaper than litigation later.
