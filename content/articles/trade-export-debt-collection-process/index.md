# 外贸货款追收全流程：从催款函到仲裁裁决

一笔货款从"逾期"到"回款"，中间有多个节点。每个节点该做什么、做到什么程度就该升级手段，是追收成败的分水岭。本文按时间顺序梳理外贸货款追收的全流程，帮您建立一张"追收地图"。

## 阶段一：内部催收（逾期0–30天）

逾期后前30天，通常是内部催收窗口：

- 先核查：是否因单据不符、质量争议、物流延误导致客户拒付——先排除客户"有理拒付"的情况；
- 分级催收：礼貌提醒→正式邮件→电话，留下完整沟通记录；
- 要求客户书面说明原因和预计付款时间——**书面确认是重要的时效证据**。

这一阶段的目标不是吵架，而是确认"客户是否有付款意愿"，并留下证据。

## 阶段二：升级为律师函（逾期30–90天）

超过30天仍未解决，建议升级为律师函。律师函的价值：

- 表明"此事已进入法律程序轨道"，促使客户认真对待；
- 中断诉讼时效，保留法律权利；
- 为后续仲裁/诉讼固定书面证据。

律师函通常给出7–15天宽限期。若客户回复并给出可行方案，可转入和解谈判；若无视或拖延，进入下一阶段。

## 阶段三：和解谈判（律师函后1–2个月）

和解不等于让步。好的和解方案应该是：分期+担保、部分减免换取一次性支付、以货抵债+差价补足。谈判要点：

- 所有方案书面化，由对方有权签字人签署；
- 优先争取担保（母公司保证、个人担保、资产抵押）；
- 明确违约加速条款：任何一期逾期，全额到期。

## 阶段四：仲裁或诉讼（和解失败后启动）

依据合同争议解决条款选择仲裁或诉讼：

- **仲裁**：裁决依据《纽约公约》在160多个缔约国可申请执行，是跨境贸易的主流选择；费用相对高但一裁终局、保密；
- **诉讼**：在合同约定法院起诉，程序公开；判决执行依赖两国之间的互惠或条约安排。

启动前建议同步申请**财产保全**（冻结账户、查封货物），防止客户转移资产。保全通常需要提供担保或初步证据，各法域规则不同。

## 阶段五：裁决/判决执行

胜诉后的执行是另一场战役：

- 境内资产：委托当地律师执行，核心是找到资产线索（账户、房产、股权）；
- 跨境执行：向资产所在地法院申请承认与执行，周期通常6–24个月；
- 执行不能时：调查债务人是否通过关联公司、亲属转移资产，必要时追究相关责任。

## 各阶段周期与成本参考

| 阶段 | 触发条件 | 通常周期 | 成本量级 |
|------|---------|---------|---------|
| 内部催收 | 逾期0–30天 | 1个月 | 内部人力 |
| 律师函 | 逾期30–90天 | 1–2周 | 低（固定费） |
| 和解谈判 | 律师函后 | 1–2个月 | 中（律师时费） |
| 仲裁/诉讼 | 和解失败 | 3–12个月 | 高（律师费+程序费） |
| 执行 | 胜诉后 | 6–24个月 | 中高（执行+调查费） |

以上为经验区间，实际以案情与法域为准。

## 外贸企业最常见的三个误区

1. **拖过时效**：客户"口头承诺"就无限等待，错过诉讼时效窗口；
2. **证据散乱**：只保留合同和发票，丢失沟通记录、对账单，导致举证困难；
3. **单打独斗**：不借助律师函和保全工具，与客户"打持久战"，最终人财两空。

# International Debt Collection: From Demand Letter to Arbitral Award

Between "overdue" and "recovered" there are several decision points. Knowing what to do at each stage — and when to escalate — is what separates successful recoveries from dead debts. This article maps the full export debt collection pathway.

## Stage 1: Internal follow-up (days 0–30)

The first 30 days are usually the internal collection window:

- Check first whether the buyer has a legitimate reason not to pay: discrepant documents, quality disputes, or logistics delays;
- Escalate politely: reminder email → formal email → phone call, keeping a complete communication record;
- Ask the buyer for a written reason and a payment date — **a written acknowledgment is critical limitation evidence**.

The goal here is not confrontation; it is determining whether the buyer intends to pay, and preserving evidence.

## Stage 2: Escalate to a demand letter (days 30–90)

If the matter is unresolved after 30 days, escalate to a lawyer's letter. Its value:

- Signals that the matter has entered a legal track, prompting the buyer to take it seriously;
- Interrupts the limitation period and preserves legal rights;
- Fixes documentary evidence for later proceedings.

A demand letter typically allows 7–15 days. If the buyer responds with a workable proposal, move to settlement talks; if ignored, escalate.

## Stage 3: Settlement negotiation (1–2 months after the letter)

Settlement is not surrender. A good deal is: instalments plus security, a partial discount for immediate payment, or payment in goods with a balancing amount. Key points:

- Put everything in writing, signed by someone with authority;
- Push for security (parent guarantee, personal guarantee, asset collateral);
- Include an acceleration clause: any missed instalment makes the full amount due.

## Stage 4: Arbitration or litigation

Choose the path per your contract's dispute resolution clause:

- **Arbitration**: awards are enforceable in 160+ New York Convention states — the mainstream choice in trade; higher cost, but final and confidential;
- **Litigation**: sue in the agreed court; proceedings are public; enforcement depends on reciprocity or treaties.

File for **asset preservation** (freezing accounts, seizing goods) at the same time to stop asset movement. Rules on security and evidence vary by jurisdiction.

## Stage 5: Enforcement of the award or judgment

Winning is only the beginning:

- Domestic assets: use local counsel; the core task is locating executable assets (accounts, property, equity);
- Cross-border: apply to the courts where assets sit for recognition and enforcement, typically 6–24 months;
- If execution fails: investigate whether the debtor moved assets through affiliates or relatives, and pursue liability where possible.

## Timeline and cost reference

| Stage | Trigger | Typical duration | Cost level |
|-------|---------|------------------|------------|
| Internal follow-up | days 0–30 | 1 month | internal |
| Demand letter | days 30–90 | 1–2 weeks | low (fixed) |
| Settlement | after letter | 1–2 months | medium (hourly) |
| Arbitration / litigation | failed settlement | 3–12 months | high |
| Enforcement | after winning | 6–24 months | medium-high |

Experience ranges; actuals depend on the facts and jurisdiction.

## Three common exporter mistakes

1. **Missing the limitation window** by waiting on verbal promises;
2. **Scattered evidence** — keeping only the contract and invoice, losing communications and statements;
3. **Fighting alone** — skipping the demand letter and preservation tools, then outlasting the client into a dead debt.
