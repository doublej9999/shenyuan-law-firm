# 商业欺诈的常见类型：合同/投资/出口骗局

一家做外贸的公司，接到一笔"大订单"，对方催着发货、催着收定金，货发出后对方连公司都不存在；一位投资人被高回报项目吸引，前期按时拿到"收益"，最后连本金都搭进去。商业欺诈的共同点：等你发现被骗时，钱往往已经被转移。识别骗局、固定证据、尽早行动，是挽回损失的三道闸门。本文梳理三类最常见的跨境商业欺诈，帮助你在交易前识别风险、在事发后正确应对。

## 一、合同类欺诈：假合同、空壳公司与伪造印章

- **空壳公司签约**：对方注册时间短、无实际经营地址，签完合同收完定金即失联；
- **伪造公章/签名**：冒用知名公司名义签约收款，事后真公司否认；
- **阴阳合同**：约定条款与口头承诺不一致，付款后才发现货不对板；
- 识别要点：查询对方注册信息与实际控制人，核验邮箱域名与官网是否一致，对"先付款后看货"保持警惕。

## 二、投资类欺诈：高回报与庞氏骗局

- **"保本高息"**：回报率明显高于市场水平（如年化远高于10%–15%且无风险披露），基本可判定异常；
- **庞氏骗局**：用后来者的本金支付前期收益，早期"按时返利"正是诱饵；
- **虚假项目**：编造海外矿产、房产、基金项目，出示伪造的批文和审计报告；
- 识别要点：查项目方资质与资金是否托管，拒绝"拉人头返佣"结构，对无法解释的高收益保持怀疑。

## 三、出口类欺诈：假客户、钓鱼邮件与伪造单据

- **假客户下单**：自称大公司采购，要求先发货后付款（O/A），货到后拒付或消失；
- **钓鱼邮件（BEC）**：冒充供应商邮箱，通知"收款账户已变更"，把货款骗到骗子账户；
- **伪造提单/质检报告**：收货方与承运人合谋无单放货，或伪造检验报告拒付；
- 识别要点：收款账户任何变更必须电话二次确认，对新客户做背景与资信调查，大额订单坚持信用证等可控结算方式。

## 发现被骗后的标准动作（尽量在48小时内）

1. **证据保全**：邮件、聊天记录、合同、转账凭证全部固定，必要时办理公证；
2. **止付**：立即联系银行申请止付/冻结，跨境汇款追回的成功率随时间快速下降；
3. **刑事报案**：向公安机关报案（达到立案标准的涉案金额），同时保留民事追偿路径；
4. **资产调查与保全**：调查对方账户、房产、股权，向法院申请冻结；
5. **律师介入**：评估管辖与诉讼时效，决定刑事、民事并行还是仲裁。

## 时间与费用预期

| 动作 | 通常周期 | 主要成本 |
|------|---------|---------|
| 证据保全与止付 | 1–3天 | 公证费，较低 |
| 刑事报案 | 立案审查约1–2个月 | 基本免费 |
| 民事/仲裁程序 | 6–18个月 | 律师费+诉讼/仲裁费 |
| 资产调查 | 2–8周 | 调查费 |

以上为经验区间。欺诈案件追回难度大、不确定性高，我们不对任何案件结果作出承诺——早期介入是唯一能稳定提升概率的因素，具体路径以当地法律和专业意见为准。

## 风险提示

- 不要轻信"有关系、能快速追回"的中介，谨防二次诈骗；
- 不要因对方"正在筹钱还款"而拖延报案，资金转移每天都在发生；
- 不要自行与骗子对峙或威胁，可能打草惊蛇并影响证据链；
- 警惕"追回后再付费"的承诺，正规律师不会承诺结果。

# Types of Commercial Fraud: Contracts, Investments, Exports

A trading company lands a "big order." The buyer urges shipment and demands a deposit — and after the goods ship, the company turns out not to exist. An investor is drawn to a high-yield project, enjoys a few punctual early "returns," and eventually loses the principal. The common thread: by the time you realize you have been defrauded, the money has usually already moved. Spotting the scheme, preserving evidence, and acting early are the three gates to recovery. This guide walks through the three most common categories of cross-border commercial fraud so you can spot the risk before you trade and respond correctly after the fact.

## 1. Contract fraud: shell companies, forged seals, phantom orders

- **Shell-company contracts**: the counterparty is newly registered with no real business address; after taking the deposit it disappears;
- **Forged seals and signatures**: fraudsters sign in the name of a reputable company and collect payment; the real company denies everything;
- **Split contracts**: the signed terms differ from the verbal promises, and the goods never match the deal;
- Red flags: check the registration and ultimate owners, verify the email domain against the official website, and be wary of "pay first, inspect later" arrangements.

## 2. Investment fraud: guaranteed returns and Ponzi schemes

- **"Guaranteed high returns"**: returns far above market levels (for example, annualized figures well beyond 10–15%) with no risk disclosure are abnormal by definition;
- **Ponzi schemes**: early investors are paid with later investors' principal; punctual early "interest" is the bait;
- **Fake projects**: fabricated overseas mines, real estate, or funds backed by forged approvals and audit reports;
- Red flags: verify licenses and fund custody, reject referral-commission structures, and stay suspicious of returns you cannot explain.

## 3. Export fraud: fake buyers, phishing emails, forged documents

- **Fake buyers**: posing as large corporate purchasers, they demand open-account terms, take the goods, and refuse to pay;
- **Business email compromise (BEC)**: fraudsters impersonate your supplier and announce a "changed bank account" to redirect payments;
- **Forged bills of lading and inspection reports**: collusion to release cargo without a bill of lading, or fabricated inspection reports to justify non-payment;
- Red flags: confirm any account change by phone with a known contact, run background and credit checks on new customers, and insist on controlled settlement methods such as letters of credit for large orders.

## Standard steps after you discover fraud (within 48 hours if possible)

1. **Preserve evidence**: emails, chat logs, contracts, and transfer receipts — secured and, if necessary, notarized;
2. **Stop payment**: contact the bank immediately to freeze or recall the transfer; recovery odds drop quickly with time;
3. **File a criminal complaint** with the police where the amount meets the threshold, while keeping the civil route open;
4. **Investigate and preserve assets**: locate accounts, property, and equity, and apply to a court to freeze them;
5. **Engage counsel**: assess jurisdiction and limitation periods, and decide between criminal, civil, or arbitration proceedings.

## Time and cost expectations

| Action | Typical duration | Main costs |
|--------|------------------|------------|
| Evidence and stop-payment | 1–3 days | Notary fees, low |
| Criminal complaint | 1–2 months review | Essentially free |
| Civil / arbitration proceedings | 6–18 months | Counsel + court/tribunal fees |
| Asset investigation | 2–8 weeks | Investigation fees |

These are experience ranges. Fraud recovery is difficult and uncertain, and we make no promise of results for any case — early intervention is the only factor that reliably improves the odds. Specific paths depend on local law and professional advice.

## Key risks

- Do not trust intermediaries who claim "connections" and "quick recovery" — you may be defrauded a second time;
- Do not delay reporting because the fraudster says they are "raising the money" — assets move every day;
- Do not confront or threaten the fraudster yourself — it can tip them off and damage the evidence chain;
- Be wary of "pay only on recovery" promises — reputable lawyers do not promise outcomes.
