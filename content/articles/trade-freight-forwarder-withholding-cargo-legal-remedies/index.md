# 货代扣单、扣货，货主如何维权？

货柜已经到港一周了，客户天天催交货，可你的货代就是不放提单——理由只有一个：上一票的运费、垫资还没结清。眼看信用证装运期马上到期，合同违约金每天都在滚，你在酒店里急得团团转。货代扣单、扣货看似只是"讨债手段"，但一旦涉及鲜活货、交期紧的订单，代价可能远超那笔运费本身。先冷静判断：哪一步合法、哪一步要见好就收，再决定走哪条路。

## 第一步：搞清楚对方扣的到底是什么

货代可能有三类行为，法律性质完全不同，应对也不同：

- **扣提单（含电放）**：如果提单运费"预付"且你已付清，货代无权以他方欠费为由扣你的正本提单或电放——这是债权凭证层面的扣留，举证相对容易；
- **扣货（留置权）**：货代对在途或到港货物主张留置，通常主张依据是《中华人民共和国民法典》关于承运人对相关费用的留置权，以及双方货运代理协议中的约定；
- **扣单证（核销单、原产地证等）**：目的多为逼迫结清历史款项，性质接近"以单证相要挟"。

先调出合同，逐条核对双方关于运费、垫资、账期和留置权的约定，这决定你谈判的底牌。

## 第二步：先走理性谈判，留痕清楚

- 正面沟通，先确认对方主张的具体金额与依据，是否包含争议费用（如你根本未确认的抬价、附加费）；
- 若对方主张有据，可提议"支付无争议部分＋对有争议部分提供担保"，换取先放单放货；
- 所有沟通走书面（邮件/微信并截图存档），明确要求对方说明扣货的法律依据，避免口头扯皮。

## 第三步：评估是否为"不当扣留"，再决定是否施压

- 若你已付清相关费用、对方属无据扣留，可发出正式函件要求限期放行，并声明产生的滞期费、仓储费、客户索赔由对方承担；
- 涉及鲜活货、冷柜货或临期订单，可主张紧急减损，必要时通过法院申请诉前保全/先予执行，要求责令放货；
- 若对方持"留置权"对抗，需专业意见判断其主张是否成立、范围是否过度。

## 第四步：行政与司法救济

- 向案发地交通/港航管理部门或行业协会投诉（效果因地区而异，不作为主要指望）；
- 通过诉讼主张返还货物或赔偿损失；货已放但造成损失的，主张违约或侵权赔偿；
- 若货在海外港口被扣，须评估当地法律对货运代理留置权的规定——不同法域差异很大，以当地法律及专业意见为准。

## 时间与费用预期

| 路径 | 经验周期 | 主要成本 |
|------|---------|---------|
| 沟通协商放货 | 数天至2周 | 低（时间成本） |
| 发函施压/行业协会投诉 | 1–3周 | 低 |
| 诉前保全/先予执行 | 2–6周 | 保全担保费+律师费 |
| 返还货物/损害赔偿诉讼 | 3–12个月，视法域而定 | 律师费+诉讼费 |

## 风险提示

- 不要在未核实金额前一怒之下拒绝支付全部款项，可能正中争议费用拖延的下怀；
- 不要口头"骂战"或撂狠话，措辞激进可能激化矛盾、把战线拖长；
- 不要因对方扣单就拒付新票运费，造成新一笔违约叠加；
- 涉及海外港口扣货时，切勿仅凭中国法律判断——必须结合货物所在地法律。

# Freight Forwarder Withholding Cargo: Legal Remedies

Your container arrived at port a week ago, the customer is calling daily for delivery, but the forwarder will not release the bill of lading — citing unpaid freight and advances from a previous shipment. The letter-of-credit shipment deadline is approaching and default penalties are mounting. Withholding the bill or the cargo may look like a routine "collection tactic," but for time-sensitive or perishable goods the true cost can dwarf the freight itself. Stay calm, determine which step is lawful, know when to compromise, and then choose your path.

## Step 1: identify what is actually being withheld

A forwarder may take three different actions, each with different legal character and response:

- **Withholding the bill of lading (including telex release)**: if the freight is "prepaid" and you have settled it, the forwarder generally has no lawful ground to withhold your original B/L to pressure unrelated unpaid fees;
- **Withholding the cargo (lien)**: the forwarder typically claims a lien on in-transit or arrived goods under the PRC Civil Code provisions on carrier liens, plus the terms of your freight-forwarding agreement;
- **Withholding documents (customs returns, certificates of origin, etc.)**: often aimed at forcing settlement of historical balances.

Pull up the contract and check clause by clause what is agreed on freight, advances, credit terms, and lien rights — it determines your negotiating position.

## Step 2: negotiate rationally, on the record

- Open a direct dialogue first to pin down the claimed amount and basis, and whether it includes disputed items (unconfirmed rate hikes or surcharges);
- Where the claim has merit, offer to pay the undisputed portion plus provide security for the disputed part in exchange for releasing the documents and cargo;
- Keep everything in writing (email or saved chat captures), and ask them to state the legal basis for withholding, to avoid oral back-and-forth.

## Step 3: assess whether the withholding is unlawful, then decide on pressure

- If you have settled the relevant charges and the retention is groundless, send a formal demand letter giving a deadline to release, and state that any demurrage, storage, or customer claims will be borne by the forwarder;
- For perishable, reefer, or deadline-driven cargo, assert urgent mitigation, and consider a court preservation or provisional order compelling release if necessary;
- If the forwarder asserts a lien, get professional advice on whether the lien stands and whether its scope is excessive.

## Step 4: administrative and judicial remedies

- File a complaint with the transport or port authority or industry association (results vary by region; do not treat this as your main lever);
- Pursue litigation to compel return of the cargo or claim damages; if the cargo has been released but loss occurred, claim for breach or tort;
- If the cargo is held at an overseas port, assess the local law on forwarder liens — rules differ significantly across jurisdictions, and local legal advice should govern.

## Time and cost expectations

| Route | Typical timeline | Main costs |
|-------|------------------|------------|
| Negotiation for release | Days to 2 weeks | Low (time) |
| Demand letter / industry complaint | 1–3 weeks | Low |
| Pre-action preservation / provisional order | 2–6 weeks | Security + counsel fees |
| Action for return or damages | 3–12 months, depending on jurisdiction | Counsel fees + court fees |

These are experience ranges, and outcomes cannot be promised. Preserving every communication and cost record is your shared foundation for both negotiation and litigation.

## Key risks

- Do not refuse to pay everything before verifying the amount — that may play into a dispute-milking strategy;
- Do not escalate into verbal confrontation; aggressive wording can prolong the fight;
- Do not withhold payment on new shipments because of this dispute, adding another breach;
- When cargo is held at an overseas port, do not judge the case purely on PRC law — the law of the cargo's location governs.
