# 父母在国内的房产，海外子女如何继承？

父母在国内的老宅、学区房，人在海外的你却不知道怎么把它们过户到自己名下。回国一趟千头万绪，听人说还要公证、认证、"涉外继承"一堆手续，拖得越久，房子可能被占用、甚至出现其他亲属主张权利的风险。跨境继承听起来复杂，但只要你按法定顺序走对流程，是可以稳妥把国内房产落到自己名下的。关键在于搞清楚适用哪国法律、需要哪些材料、公证认证怎么弄，以及如何避免"权利凭证拿到了、房子却过不了户"的尴尬。

## 第一步：确定适用法律与继承人范围

- 中国法律下，被继承人死亡时经常居所地法律决定继承，但**不动产继承适用不动产所在地法律**，所以国内房产一般适用中国法律；\\
- 合法继承人通常包括配偶、子女、父母（第一顺序），以及兄弟姐妹、祖父母、外祖父母（第二顺序）；\\
- 若被继承人有遗嘱，先看遗嘱是否有效、是否涉及特留份等中国法限制，以当地法律与专业意见为准。

## 第二步：办理继承权公证（关键一步）

- 所有继承人（含境外的你）通常需共同到房产所在地公证处，或委托代理人办理继承权公证；\\
- 需提供被继承人死亡证明、房产证、亲属关系证明、继承人身份与护照材料；\\
- 海外形成的文书（如死亡证明、亲属关系证明、婚姻/出生证明）通常需经当地公证、海牙认证或中国使领馆认证，并提供中文翻译件。

## 第三步：办理房产过户

- 凭继承权公证书、身份证件与房产证，到不动产登记机构申请转移登记；\\
- 存在多个继承人的，可协商份额，共同办理或经协商确定由一人继承；\\
- 过户时一般会涉及印花税、契税等，具体以当地政策为准，如有减免规定应一并核对。

## 时间与费用预期

| 环节 | 经验周期 | 主要成本 |
|------|---------|---------|
| 海外文书公证/认证与翻译 | 数周至数月 | 公证认证费+翻译费 |
| 继承权公证办理 | 数周 | 公证费（按标的） |
| 房产过户登记 | 数周 | 登记费+税费 |

以上均为经验区间，取决于文书的齐全程度、是否需海牙认证及当地登记效率。**我们不承诺结果**，但材料越齐全、继承人能协同，整体时间越可控。

## 风险提示

- 海外文书认证不全，是"过不了户"最常见的原因，务必逐项核对；\\
- 若其他继承人失联或不愿配合，可能需要诉讼确认继承权，周期更长；\\
- 房产可能涉及共有、抵押或限购等情形，了解清楚再办理；\\
- 涉及中国法与外国法的衔接部分，以当地法律与专业意见为准。

# Overseas Children Inheriting Parents' Property in China

Your parents left a family home or an apartment in mainland China, and you live overseas. You are not sure how to transfer it into your name. Returning home feels overwhelming—people mention notarisation, authentication, and a pile of "foreign-related inheritance" red tape—and the longer you wait, the greater the risk of the property being occupied or other relatives asserting claims. Cross-border inheritance sounds complicated, but if you follow the lawful steps in order, you can settle the mainland property in your name. The key is knowing which law applies, what documents you need, how notarisation and authentication work, and how to avoid the "certificate in hand, property untransferable" dead end.

## Step 1: determine applicable law and heirs

- Under Chinese law, inheritance of a deceased's personal estate follows the law of habitual residence at death, but **immovable property is governed by the law of the place where it is located**, so mainland real estate is generally subject to Chinese law; \\
- Legal heirs usually include the spouse, children and parents (first order), plus siblings and grandparents (second order); \\
- If there is a will, first check whether it is valid and whether it implicates Chinese rules such as reserved portions; follow local law and professional advice.

## Step 2: handle inheritance notarisation (the key step)

- All heirs, including yourself overseas, usually need to obtain inheritance notarisation at the notary office where the property is located, or authorise an agent; \\
- Provide the death certificate, title deed, kinship certificates, and heirs' identity and passport documents; \\
- Documents formed overseas (death, kinship, marriage/birth certificates) usually need local notarisation, apostille or Chinese consulate authentication, plus Chinese translation.

## Step 3: complete the property transfer

- Apply for transfer registration at the real estate registration authority using the inheritance certificate, ID and title deed; \\
- With multiple heirs, agree on shares and either register jointly or choose one heir after consultation; \\
- Stamp duty and deed tax may apply on transfer; check local policy and any exemptions.

## Time and cost expectations

| Stage | Typical timeline | Main costs |
|-------|------------------|------------|
| Overseas notarisation/authentication and translation | Weeks to months | Notarisation/authentication fees + translation |
| Inheritance notarisation | Weeks | Notary fee (based on value) |
| Property transfer registration | Weeks | Registration fee + taxes |

These are experience ranges, depending on document completeness, whether apostille is needed, and local registration efficiency. **We do not promise results**, but complete documents and cooperating heirs keep the timeline more manageable.

## Key risks

- Incomplete authentication of overseas documents is the most common reason transfer stalls—check each item carefully; \\
- If other heirs are missing or refuse to cooperate, litigation to confirm heirship may be needed, which takes longer; \\
- The property may be co-owned, mortgaged, or subject to purchase restrictions—understand these before acting; \\
- Follow local law and professional advice where Chinese and foreign law interact.
