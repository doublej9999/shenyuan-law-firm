# 查到资产后的保全动作：冻结令与禁止令

你费尽周折终于查到欠款人名下的存款、房产或应收账款，还没来得及高兴，就听说他正在把财产转让、转移资金。追收最怕的就是"查到却锁不住"——刚查到，资产下一秒就换了地方，之前的功夫全白费。资产保全动作（冻结令、禁止令、扣押令）的价值就在于此：在债务转移的窗口期把资产"定住"。它不是等到赢了官司才做的事，而是查到资产后就要抢时间申请的紧急措施。用得好，能让债务人想动也动不了，为后续追收和执行留住可能的清偿来源。

## 第一步：判断是否需要且能否申请保全

当存在资产被转移、贬值或被处置的风险时，可考虑申请保全。多数法域的冻结令（freezing injunction）或境内财产保全，通常要求：有可主张的债权或诉由、存在资产灭失或转移的紧迫风险，并常须提供担保（如保证金）。先请律师对照当地规则评估门槛，避免凭直觉空跑一趟。

## 第二步：选择适合的保全形式

- **冻结令/禁止令（Freezing/Mareva injunction）**：禁止债务人在额度内处分资产，常用于银行账户、应收账款等金钱性财产；\\
- **财产保全/扣押令**：对特定房产、货物、股权等的查封、扣押或禁止过户，阻断转让登记；\\
- **临时禁令**：在判决前维持现状、防止进一步损害；\\
- **婚姻/其他禁令**（视案情）：按具体法律关系灵活选用。

## 第三步：申请保全的操作要点

- **及时性与保密性**：保全常要求"紧急且单方申请"，先防止打草惊蛇，避免债务人闻风转移；\\
- **证据与担保准备**：准备好权利证明、资产线索与风险说明，并按法院要求提供担保；\\
- **明确保全范围与法域**：列明拟保全的资产与金额上限，跨境资产需在对应法域分别申请；\\
- **衔接后续执行**：保全往往与诉讼/仲裁、执行程序配套，需同步推进取得执行依据。

## 第四步：保全后的跟进与风险

获得保全不等于拿到钱，还需继续诉讼或仲裁并取得可执行依据，再进入执行程序。同时注意保全并非永久，可能因未及时推进诉讼、担保不足或程序瑕疵被解除，需由律师按时维持并更新申请。

## 时间与费用预期

| 环节 | 经验周期 | 主要成本 |
|------|---------|---------|
| 评估与证据准备 | 数天至数周 | 律师费 |

## 风险提示

- 保全通常是"前置"动作，需与诉讼/仲裁及执行衔接，别只保不诉；\\
- 打草惊蛇或程序不当可能导致保全被解除甚至需赔偿损失；\\
- 境外保全规则差异大，以当地法律与专业意见为准；\\
- 担保与续期成本不可忽视，先评估整体预算再决定是否申请。

# Freezing Injunctions and Asset Preservation Orders

You finally located the debtor's bank deposits, property, or receivables—only to hear he is transferring assets and moving money. The worst outcome in collection is "found but not secured": the asset you located changes hands the next day and all your work is wasted. Asset preservation measures—freezing injunctions, prohibitory orders, and seizure orders—exist precisely to "pin" assets during the window before transfer. This is not something to do after you win; it is an urgent measure to request as soon as you locate the assets. Done right, it can stop the debtor from moving anything and preserve a possible source of satisfaction for the eventual recovery and enforcement.

## Step 1: decide whether preservation applies and can be granted

Where there is a risk that assets will be transferred, devalued, or disposed of, preservation may be warranted. In most jurisdictions, a freezing injunction or domestic asset preservation typically requires: a claim or cause of action, an imminent risk of dissipation or removal, and often security (such as a bond or payment into court). Have counsel check the local threshold first so you do not act on intuition and come away empty-handed.

## Step 2: choose the right form of preservation

- **Freezing injunction (Mareva)**: forbids the debtor from disposing of assets up to a stated amount—often used for bank accounts, receivables, and other monetary assets;\\
- **Asset preservation/seizure order**: attaches or freezes specific property, goods, or shares and blocks transfer or registration;\\
- **Interim injunction**: preserves the status quo and prevents further harm before judgment;\\
- **Other injunctions**: tailored to the particular legal relationship as the facts require.

## Step 3: practical points in obtaining preservation

- **Act promptly and confidentially**: preservation is often urgent and applied for ex parte, to avoid tipping off the debtor and letting assets move;\\
- **Prepare evidence and security**: ready proof of your right, asset leads, and the risk explanation, and provide any security the court requires;\\
- **Define the scope and jurisdiction**: list the assets and a monetary cap; cross-border assets usually require separate applications in each jurisdiction;\\
- **Link to later enforcement**: preservation usually runs alongside litigation/arbitration and enforcement, so advance the underlying claim in parallel.

## Step 4: follow-up and risks after preservation

Obtaining a freeze is not the same as getting paid. You must continue the litigation or arbitration and obtain an enforceable basis before moving to execution. Note that preservation is not permanent—it can be discharged for failing to pursue the claim in time, insufficient security, or procedural defects, so counsel should maintain and renew the order.

## Time and cost expectations

| Stage | Typical timeline | Main costs |
|-------|------------------|------------|
| Assessment and evidence preparation | Days to weeks | Legal fees |
| Applying for the order | Days to weeks (faster if urgent) | Court costs + security |
| Maintenance and renewal | Depends on jurisdiction | Legal fees |
| Linking to enforcement | Weeks to months | Enforcement costs |

These are experience ranges; figures depend on jurisdiction, asset type, and urgency. **We do not promise results**, but the shorter the gap between "finding" and "securing," the better the odds of recovery.

## Key risks

- Preservation is usually a "front-end" move—link it to litigation/arbitration and enforcement; do not just freeze and stop;\\
- Tipping off the debtor or procedural errors can lead to discharge and even liability for damages;\\
- Rules abroad vary widely—follow local law and professional advice;\\
- Do not underestimate security and renewal costs; assess the overall budget before applying.
