# 美国判决在中国法院申请承认与执行

你在美国打赢了官司，判决写得清清楚楚，可债务人把资产都挪到了中国——上海的账户、深圳的房产。如果这份美国判决在中国"不认"，赢的只是一张纸，钱照样拿不回来。中国法院承认与执行外国法院判决，不是自动的，而是依据中华人民共和国与判决国之间的互惠关系或司法协助条约做出；对美国判决，两国并无专门条约，实践中能否获承认往往因案而异、存在不确定性。正因如此，申请承认与执行对程序合规和证据准备要求极高，一步出错就可能前功尽弃。下面给出可执行的路径。

## 第一步：先评估"能不能认、值不值得认"

- 启动前先评估互惠基础与判例：同院/同地区法院此前是否承认过美国判决；\
- 大多数情形需向中国有管辖权的中级人民法院提出申请，能否受理与获认取决于个案；\
- 结合债务人资产所在地与可执行性，判断申请的现实价值与成本。

## 第二步：备齐申请材料并做公证认证

- 美国判决书及其翻译、证明文件，通常需经当地公证及相应领事认证（apostille/认证），并由有资质机构翻译为中文；\
- 准备美国法院的程序完备证明（判决已生效、可执行、已合法送达被告等）；\
- 证明债务人确有位于中国境内的可执行财产。

## 第三步：向管辖法院提出申请

- 依《中华人民共和国民事诉讼法》，向被执行人住所地或财产所在地的中级人民法院提出承认与执行申请；\
- 中国法院仅对判决的终局性、程序正当与不违反中国法律基本原则等进行审查，不重新审理实体争议；\
- 审查期间配合法院要求补充材料，必要时委托中国律师对接。

## 第四步：获承认后的执行

- 法院裁定承认并准予执行后，进入与中国生效判决相同的执行程序，可申请冻结账户、查封房产等；\
- 若执行中遇到转移资产，及时申请财产保全，锁定执行成果。

## 时间与费用预期

| 环节 | 经验周期 | 主要成本 |
|------|---------|---------|
| 互惠与价值评估 | 数周 | 咨询/评估费 |
| 公证、认证与翻译 | 数周至数月 | 公证认证费+翻译费 |
| 法院审查与裁定 | 通常数月，个案差异大 | 申请费+中国律师费 |
| 获准后强制执行 | 数月，视资产 | 执行费+律师费 |

以上均为经验区间，取决于案件复杂程度、法院办案节奏与债务配合程度。**我们不承诺结果**，美国判决能否在华获承认与执行存在不确定性，务必以个案为准。

## 风险提示

- 切勿假设"美国判决当然在中国有效"——是否获承认取决于互惠与个案审查；\
- 材料未经合法公证认证或翻译不规范，可能被退回；\
- 中国法律与美国法律对送达、终局性等要求不同，拿不准的以当地法律/专业意见为准；\
- 谨防"保证执行成功"的说法，必要时要求律师书面说明依据与不确定性。

# Enforcing US Judgments in Chinese Courts

You won in the United States—judgment in hand—but the debtor has parked its assets in China: a Shanghai account, a Shenzhen apartment. If Chinese courts refuse to recognise the US judgment, you have won only a piece of paper and the money stays out of reach. Recognition and enforcement of foreign judgments in China is not automatic; it rests on reciprocity or a judicial-assistance treaty, and there is no dedicated treaty with the United States. In practice, recognition is assessed case by case and carries real uncertainty. That is why the application demands careful procedural compliance and evidence preparation—one misstep and it all falls apart. Here is an actionable path.

## Step 1: assess whether it is recognisable and worth pursuing

- Before filing, assess the reciprocity basis and precedent: has this court or region recognised a US judgment before?\
- In most cases you apply to the competent intermediate people's court in China; acceptance and recognition depend on the particular case;\
- Weigh the debtor's asset location and enforceability against the cost and value of applying.

## Step 2: prepare and legalise your documents

- The US judgment, its translation, and supporting documents typically require local notarization and consular legalisation (apostille/certification), professionally translated into Chinese;\
- Prepare proof of finality and procedural soundness of the US judgment (final, enforceable, proper service on the defendant);\
- Show that the debtor holds enforceable assets located in China.

## Step 3: file with the competent court

- Under the PRC Civil Procedure Law, file for recognition and enforcement with the intermediate people's court at the debtor's domicile or where the assets sit;\
- Chinese courts review finality, procedural fairness, and consistency with fundamental Chinese law, rather than retrying the merits;\
- Cooperate with the court's requests for supplementary documents and instruct Chinese counsel to liaise during review.

## Step 4: enforce once recognised

- After the court recognises and grants enforcement, the case proceeds like any domestic Chinese judgment—account freezes and property seizures become available;\
- If assets are being moved, apply promptly for preservation to secure the enforcement result.

## Time and cost expectations

| Stage | Typical timeline | Main costs |
|-------|------------------|------------|
| Reciprocity and value assessment | Weeks | Consultation/evaluation fee |
| Notarization, legalisation, translation | Weeks to months | Notary fees + translation |
| Court review and ruling | Usually months, case-dependent | Filing fees + Chinese counsel |
| Post-recognition enforcement | Months, by assets | Enforcement + counsel fees |

These are experience ranges, depending on complexity, court pace, and debtor cooperation. **We do not promise results**—whether a US judgment is recognised and enforced in China carries uncertainty and must be assessed case by case.

## Key risks

- Do not assume a US judgment is automatically valid in China—recognition depends on reciprocity and case review;\
- Materials without proper legalisation or standard translation may be rejected;\
- Chinese and US law differ on service and finality; when unsure, follow local law and professional advice;\
- Beware "guaranteed enforcement": ask counsel to set out the legal basis and uncertainties in writing.
