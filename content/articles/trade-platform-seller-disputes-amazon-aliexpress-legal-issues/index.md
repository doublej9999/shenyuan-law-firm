# 跨境电商平台（亚马逊/速卖通）商家合同纠纷处理

一夜之间店铺被封、货款被冻结、库存压在海外仓，买家批量退款、跟卖抢走了你的 Listing——在亚马逊、速卖通上做生意，这些"平台纠纷"随时可能发生。最难受的是：**你和平台之间没有平等谈判的资格，规则几乎都是平台单方制定**。懂规则、会固定证据、知道何时升级，是商家保护自己最实际的能力。

## 先分清：这是平台内部纠纷，还是可诉法律纠纷

- **平台内部违规处理**（封店、冻结、移除 Listing、差评影响排名）：本质是平台依据其**服务协议与卖家政策**作出的决定，通常先走平台内部申诉通道；
- **买家端纠纷**（恶意退款、退货调包、拒收）：可能涉及买卖双方的违约责任，平台调解是第一步，但可上升到法律索赔；
- **第三方侵权/抢注**（跟卖、抢注商标、仿冒劫持）：可能构成侵权或违约，需要法律手段而非仅靠平台申诉。

先给纠纷定性，才能选对处理路径——**多数情况下，平台申诉在先，法律程序在后，但两者并不互斥**。

## 分步骤处理路径

**1. 立即固定与备份证据。** 抓取并保存店铺后台记录、订单、物流、聊天、买家反馈、平台通知与政策页面。**截图要带时间与账号标识**，并备份到本地，防止账号被封后数据丢失。

**2. 仔细阅读平台的"卖家政策/销售政策"。** 封店与冻结通常对应具体条款（如绩效指标、知识产权投诉、账户关联）。把平台引用的条款调出来，逐条对应你的实际情况。

**3. 走完内部申诉流程。** 按平台指定途径提交 POA（行动计划）、证据与说明。申诉材料要**聚焦违规点与整改方案**，而非泛泛辩解。

**4. 评估升级路径。** 若平台申诉无果，或纠纷涉及买家/第三方的真实违约或侵权，可评估：平台服务协议约定的**仲裁条款**（亚马逊/速卖通卖家协议常含仲裁约定与管辖）、当地协商、以及必要的法律索赔。

**5. 分清管辖与适用法。** 平台协议的适用法常为卢森堡、香港或平台总部所在地法律。**能否起诉、去哪起诉，以协议与当地法律为准**，必要时由懂跨境电商与两地法律的律师评估。

## 风险提示

- **别删改或新开账号"绕"平台**：可能被视为账户关联违规，加重处理；
- **别忽视仲裁条款**：不少平台协议强制仲裁并对启动方式设限，直接起诉可能被驳回；
- **别只靠客服聊天留证**：平台聊天记录未必是合格证据，核心数据要完整备份；
- **别把平台规则当法律**：合规申诉与法律权利是两回事，诉求要分开表述。

在平台上做跨境生意，**合规与证据意识就是护城河**。纠纷发生时，先守证据、再走流程、最后才谈法律——这个次序能为你争取最大的主动权。

# Platform Seller Disputes: Amazon & AliExpress Legal Issues

Overnight your store is suspended, funds frozen, inventory stuck in an overseas warehouse, buyers mass-refunding and hijackers taking your listings — doing business on Amazon or AliExpress means these "platform disputes" can hit any time. The hard part: **you are not an equal negotiator with the platform, and the rules are almost entirely set by the platform unilaterally.** Knowing the rules, fixing evidence, and knowing when to escalate is the most practical skill a seller can have.

## First classify: platform-internal dispute, or a suable legal dispute

- **Platform-internal enforcement** (suspension, freezing, listing removal, ranking impact from negative feedback): essentially the platform's decision under its **seller agreement and policies** — usually start with the internal appeal channel;
- **Buyer-side disputes** (chargebacks, swapped/returned goods, refusals): may involve breach of contract on either side; platform mediation is the first step but a legal claim is possible;
- **Third-party infringement/trademark squatting** (hijacking, trademark squatting, counterfeit takeovers): may amount to infringement or breach, needing legal action rather than just an appeal.

Classifying the dispute first tells you which path to take — **in most cases appeal comes first and legal proceedings later, but they are not mutually exclusive.**

## A step-by-step approach

**1. Fix and back up evidence immediately.** Capture store-backend records, orders, logistics, chats, buyer feedback, platform notices and policy pages. **Screenshots should carry timestamps and account identifiers**, and be backed up locally in case your account is suspended and data becomes inaccessible.

**2. Read the platform's seller policy carefully.** Suspension and freezing usually link to specific clauses (performance metrics, IP complaints, account linkage). Pull out the cited clause and match it against your actual situation.

**3. Complete the internal appeal.** Submit a POA (plan of action), evidence and explanation through the designated channel. Focus the appeal on **the violation point and the remediation plan**, not vague argument.

**4. Assess escalation paths.** If the appeal fails, or the dispute involves genuine breach or infringement by a buyer or third party, evaluate: the **arbitration clause** in the platform seller agreement (Amazon/AliExpress agreements often include arbitration and a specified seat), local negotiation, and any legal claim.

**5. Sort out governing law and jurisdiction.** Platform agreements often choose the law of Luxembourg, Hong Kong or the platform's headquarters. **Whether and where you can sue depends on the agreement and local law**, best assessed by counsel familiar with both cross-border e-commerce and the two relevant legal systems.

## Risk notes

- **Do not delete data or open a new account to "work around" the platform**: this may be treated as account linkage and escalate the penalty;
- **Do not ignore the arbitration clause**: many platform agreements mandate arbitration with specific steps; filing straight to court may be dismissed;
- **Do not rely only on chat logs**: platform chat records may not be competent evidence — back up core data fully;
- **Do not equate platform rules with law**: compliance appeals and legal rights are different; keep the claims separate.

In cross-border platform business, **compliance and evidence awareness are your moat.** When a dispute hits, preserve evidence first, follow the process, and only then talk law — this order gives you the most initiative.
